Three separate pieces of our vision work, from different clips and two sports (not one end-to-end run). (a) A freely licensed real rally (Pexels, not broadcast footage; sponsor board painted out): the public TrackNet ball detector with our own offline linking and court-line fitting finds the ball in 33 of the shot's 35 frames (89% of the clip) and registers the court on every frame; no in/out call is made on this clip. (b) A test bounce of the TrackNet broadcast set (game 10; games 8-10 were kept out of our tuning, but the public detector saw frames of all ten games), in court metres: our simplest predictor (the latest tracked point projected onto the court; hollow grey: the track after the call) put the landing 1.30 m out 66 ms before the bounce at an assumed 25 fps, beyond the 35 cm margin, so we called it out; the ball (x) landed 63 cm out, so the call was right although the prediction was 69 cm off. Picked by a fixed rule: the earliest of the 3 test outs called at the 33 ms slot, all correct; only bounce-time calls passed our 95% precision rule on the training games. (c) Eq. (1) as a clock with numbers from two sports: a median 162 ms call lead (held-out table tennis, 4 of 41 misses called), our 54 ms pipeline, 65 ms of network, a simulated 1 s feed and the 1 s venue hold put the order at 1.96 s after the point: 1,116 ms after the median reprice under our pre-registered 2.0 s stamp lag and only about 24 ms before it under the post hoc 3.14 s reading (shaded: the median reprice under three stamp-lag readings, inferred, not measured).
