## 2. PSR and DSR (Bailey & López de Prado 2014)
SR\* is the expected maximum Sharpe of N zero-skill trials, √V · ((1−γ) Φ⁻¹(1−1/N) + γ Φ⁻¹(1−1/(N·e))). Three choices of V, the cross-trial variance of daily Sharpe:
- **null:** V = 1/(T−1) of the series being tested (the NOTE.md §8 convention; gives SR\* = 4.8 at T = 206, N = 3,386).
- **lowloss grid:** variance across the 24 v2-safe variants' IS Sharpes: sd 1.03 annualised, range 12.5 to 16.0.
- **sizing grid:** variance across the 55 sizing policies (res, actual fee): sd 2.98 annualised, range 4.1 to 16.8.

N = 44 is H1–H6. N = 3,386 is H1–H6 plus the six v2 lenses, per NOTE.md §8. N = 3,410 adds the 24 v2-safe grid variants, which were run after that count was taken.

How to read the table:
- **The IS DSRs are high under every variance source.** A daily Sharpe of 0.76 over 206 days clears even the largest SR\* (10.7 annualised, from the sizing grid's variance).
- **The burned-OOS DSR depends on V.** With the null variance at T = 40, the expected best of 3,386 zero-skill trials is 11.0 annualised, above v2's 6.7, so 40 days cannot rule out luck at that N. With the v2-safe grid's variance, SR\* is 3.7 and the DSR is 0.84.
- **The sizing-grid variance is the most conservative source.** Its spread mostly reflects real design differences (G against A0), not luck, so it inflates SR\*.
- **Applying N = 3,386 to the OOS is conservative.** v2 was frozen before the OOS was evaluated, so the selection was not made on these 40 days. But the OOS was later looked at (it is burned), so the conservative reading is kept alongside the N = 44 one.

## 3. PBO by CSCV (Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu 2017)
Each grid's IS daily P&L matrix (T × N) is cut into S = 16 contiguous blocks of 12–13 days. Every one of the C(16, 8) = 12,870 half/half splits is used:
- The variant with the best IS-half performance is picked.
- λ = logit(ω), where ω is that variant's relative rank in the other half, rank / (N+1), with ties averaged.
- PBO = P(λ ≤ 0).
- The degradation line is a regression of the pick's OOS-half performance on its IS-half performance.
- The control slope is the same regression for one fixed variant (the most frequent pick), with no selection at all.

How to read the table:
- **The degradation slopes are mostly the complement artifact.** They are strongly negative (-0.89 and -0.98), but the control slope for a fixed variant is almost the same (-1.00 and -0.99). The two halves split one fixed sample, so when a half gets the good days, the other half gets the rest. Selection decay shows up instead as the gap between the pick's IS and OOS means: 16.2 → 15.4 on the v2-safe grid and 16.9 → 16.8 on the sizing grid.
- **Sizing grid, PBO 0%.** The G family (risk-parity sizing, wallet filter, 0.05–0.95 zone, per-match net cap) beats the rest of the menu by a margin that no 103-day half can reverse. The IS-best is a G policy in every split, and it lands in the OOS top ranks. This says the choice of G over A–F is not a fluke of the sample. It says nothing about the onset-to-causal gap.
- **v2-safe grid, PBO 15% on Sharpe.** The 24 variants are close (sd of IS Sharpe 1.0), so their ranking is noisy. Even so, the IS-best Sharpe variant ranks above the OOS median 85% of the time. GRID.md's own rule (most profitable days, subject to the feasibility floor) has a PBO of 42%, close to a coin flip. That matches `research/v2/lowloss/RESULTS.md`: the net cap reliably cuts the size of losses but does not reliably make losing days rarer.

## 5. Minimum track record length (Bailey & López de Prado 2012)
MinTRL = 1 + (1 − γ₃·SR + (γ₄−1)/4·SR²) · (z₀.₉₅ / (SR − SR\*))², with SR the observed daily Sharpe, γ₃ the skew and γ₄ the Pearson kurtosis.

The last column uses SR\* = E[max] over 3,386 trials, with V from the wider of the two IS grid variances. That is how long a track would have to run at the observed Sharpe to beat the best of 3,386 lucky trials.

## Caveats
- **Daily P&L is not iid.** PSR, DSR and MinTRL assume iid daily returns. The lag-1 autocorrelations are in table 1, and the stationary bootstrap is the check that is robust to short-range dependence.
- **The burned OOS is non-blind.** Its 40 days were looked at before v2-safe was designed, and the last day (2026-10-03) is partial. The clean test is still the forward window.
- **The sizing grid is not the causal book.** It runs on onset-window features. Its PBO measures the lens's selection step, not the causal book.
- **CSCV halves are calendar blocks.** Each half spans different months and different fast-tier wallet sets. Some of what CSCV calls overfitting is regime change across months.
- **The DSR trial count is a count of configurations, not of independent trials.** Many of the 3,386 variants are near-duplicates, and most were scored on other metrics or other books. The effective number of independent trials is smaller, so N = 3,386 overstates the deflation.
