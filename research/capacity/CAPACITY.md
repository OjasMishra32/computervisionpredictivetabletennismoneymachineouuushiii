## The answer (one paragraph for the paper)

Simulated at a 1 s licensed-feed baseline (assumed data: licensed feed/video not purchased; parameters measured; paper only, no order sent), the CV strategy has no capacity to speak of at the pre-registered 2.0 s stamp lag: its Sharpe is 2.7 in sample and 0.8 in the burned OOS even at the smallest size, and a 1 s feed already sits at its breakeven feed delay (1.01–1.09 s). Under the post hoc estimate of the stamp lag (3.14 s, a one-day inference; 95% interval 2.23–3.22 s), with 10 covered matches a day and half the stale depth, it runs up to about $28,000–$40,000 of capital in the burned OOS and $55,000–$73,000 in sample at Sharpe ≥ 4 (where its Sharpe has halved from the smallest size), earning about $32–$68/day and $94–$183/day there. The range brackets how we share the stale depth with the faster traders already there: the upper end gives our shares the best levels of the book, the lower end a pro-rata share of every level. Capital is 3 × peak dollars locked under a 4 h lock per position, which is about what locking each position until its market closes needs (0.94–1.10×). Every fill walks the measured live book and never takes more shares than were resting. Beyond the half-Sharpe size, impact and match risk take over: correct-call edge falls from 2.4¢ a share at the smallest size to 1.8¢ at 5,000-share positions with $1k orders, wrong calls cost 3.0¢ to 3.7¢ a share, and bigger net positions per match add outcome variance faster than edge. The P&L peaks at $339/day on $219,000 in sample (Sharpe 2.0, seed band −$95 to $896) and $177/day on $83,000 in the burned OOS (Sharpe 1.3); 5,000-share positions with $1k orders make −$73/day in the burned OOS. Covering every match with a book raises the half-Sharpe capital to $65,000–$97,000 (burned OOS) and $114,000–$165,000 (in sample). Just below the half-Sharpe size our notional is 0.22% of Polymarket's in-play tennis volume, and our correct-call notional is 5% of the with-move prints in the first 3 s after each jump's onset: small next to the market, not small next to the trading around each reprice. A central $167/day data licence is covered from about $65,000 in sample and from $77,000 in the burned OOS (Sharpe 1.3 there; at no size with the pro-rata allocation); covering every match, the camera-free deployment (licence plus L4 cloud GPUs, $291–$315 a day) breaks even from about $65,000 in sample and $117,000 in the burned OOS. Depth per match has been about flat (+0.6% a month over the last six months); volume grew through more listed matches, which is the coverage dial, not deeper books. As a sensitivity with no date attached, twice the depth per match would put the half-Sharpe capital at $59,000–$99,000. For comparison, v2 at the fast tier's own fills peaks in the burned OOS at $92/day on $23,000.

**What the verifiers changed** (capacity and end-to-end verifiers, 2026-10-03; section 8 has the before/after numbers):

- *Fills never take more shares than were resting.* The stale depth is stored in dollars at the live day's prices; turning it into shares at a cheaper historical fill price created shares that never existed (2.5–17 % of correct-call shares in the first grid). Every fill is now capped at phi × the shares resting on that side at our arrival time (× the volume scale); a wrong call at the shares on its side.
- *Large orders are priced on the book they walk.* Beyond 12,800 live-book shares the first grid jumped to the whole side's value, which priced wrong calls at the 99¢ clip and inflated large-order edge. The cumulative value is now interpolated to the whole side; the wrong-call loss reported is the one actually charged.
- *How we share the stale depth is a bracket, not an assumption.* 'Best levels' (upper bracket) gives our n shares the book's first n levels and leaves the worse ones to the fast tier; 'pro rata' (lower bracket) takes phi of every level, so n shares are priced as the first n / phi. Every capacity number is quoted for both.
- *Capital is locked until the payout.* Lock-up is measured to the market's close (when the payout is available), not the match's finish; the 4 h ex-ante lock turns out about right (section 3).
- *Depth growth is a sensitivity, not an outlook.* Per-match in-play volume has been about flat; total volume grew because more matches were listed, which is the coverage dial (section 7).
- *Timing margin is not trading margin.* The end-to-end run's order is executable 2.1 s after the point, under 3 s; but at the pre-registered 2.0 s stamp lag a 1 s feed already sits at the strategy's breakeven feed delay (section 9).

**How to read 'capacity'.** Three numbers per configuration, over the size cells (net cap × $/order cap): (i) *Sharpe halves*: walking up the size path (net cap 50 → 5,000 shares with $250 orders for the CV strategy, whose cells form the efficient frontier; the frozen $1k ticket for v2), the capital where the seed-mean Sharpe has fallen to half the smallest size's, interpolated in log capital: the risk-adjusted capacity; (ii) *P&L-max*: the cell with the highest seed-mean $/day, which is where the marginal $/day of extra capital hits zero within the grid (the T-bill cost of capital, 4 % a year, is ≤ $0.11/day per $1k and moves nothing); (iii) *robust P&L-max*: the best cell whose 2.5 % seed band is still above zero. Capital = 3 × peak dollars locked with the repo's 4 h ex-ante lock. Unless a table says 'pro rata', numbers are for the best-levels allocation.

## 3. Capital: peak locked, capital required, lock-up, return, cost of capital

Positions are held to resolution. The payout is available when the market closes, which comes after the match finishes (a median of 0.53 h later, p90 2.4 h, over 6,476 delay-1 matches with both times). So the realised lock is measured to the market's close. Capital = 3 × peak dollars locked; 'to close' locks each position until its market closes; 'to finish' (the first grid's 'resolution') until the match finishes.

Along the 10/day $250 path, locking to the close needs 1.05–1.10× the capital of the 4 h lock in sample and 0.94–0.98× in the burned OOS; locking only to the finish would need 0.94–0.97× and 0.68–0.71×. Covering every match: 1.06–1.12× in sample and 0.69–0.72× in the burned OOS, where shorter OOS holds make the 4 h lock conservative. So the 4 h lock is about right at 10 matches a day; the first write-up's 'about 25 % less capital' measured the lock to the finish, not to the payout. Hold to close: median 2.0 h, p90 4.5–4.6 h in sample (1.5 h and 4.3 h OOS); 2.3–2.4 % of burned-OOS fills sit in markets that close more than 24 h later.

They do not by themselves show which limit binds; in the model the limits are the stale depth at each point (the share cap and the $ depth) and, at the larger sizes, the outcome variance of bigger net positions per match (section 1's binding columns).

## 9. Timing margin is not trading margin

The end-to-end run (`research/e2e/RESULTS.md`) makes an order executable 2,119 ms after the point with the simulated 1 s feed, so timing alone would allow a feed of up to 1.88 s (p50; 1.76 s on the worst call) under 3 s. The strategy's breakeven feed delay (stored tier-0 cells, `results/tier0/latency_sweep.json`) is 1.01 s (burned OOS) to 1.09 s (IS) at the pre-registered 2.0 s stamp lag and 2.14–2.23 s at the post hoc 3.14 s lag. So under the pre-registered reading the 1 s baseline already sits at breakeven, and every capacity number here is conditional on the post hoc 3.14 s estimate, a one-day inference. That run's conditions: CV fed at 10 frames/s, every frame (12x slow motion; the laptop cannot run 120 fps in real time); all 24 orders were timing probes: the rule and the risk check declined every call on pre-match books; its 100-share probes on pre-match books are not capacity evidence.

## Caveats

- The CV strategy is a simulation: no licensed feed or video was bought, no camera is at any court, and the 1 s feed baseline is simulated. Its timing rests on an unmeasured stamp lag; the two readings bracket it and give very different answers. At the pre-registered 2.0 s reading a 1 s feed sits at the strategy's breakeven feed delay.
- Impact is the measured live book of one day (2026-10-03, 9 WTA matches, 265 points with a >= 3c move), scaled down to smaller matches and never up (except in the depth sensitivity). Every fill is capped at the shares that were resting; depth on other days, at other tournaments or after we start trading may differ, and the fast tier may react to us (phi fixed).
- How we share the stale depth with the fast tier is not known; the best-levels and pro-rata allocations bracket it and every capacity number is given for both.
- Capital uses the repo's 4 h ex-ante lock. Locking each position until its market closes (payout available) needs about the same capital (section 3); a few percent of fills sit in markets that close more than a day later.
- v2 has no price-impact model: it never fills beyond the print it copies, so its larger rows are upper bounds; phi_v2 < 1 is the only competition dial.
- The burned OOS is non-blind (looked at before); it is reported, never used to choose a size. Seed bands are model draws, not data uncertainty.
- The volume trend is a log-linear fit to one venue's tennis tapes, descriptive only; per-match volume has been flat, so the depth-growth cells are a sensitivity with no date attached.
- The camera-free cost assumes a licensed betting-video feed costs the same as the point-data licence (no public price) and uses the L4 GPU price; T4 throughput at 120 fps is not measured.
- The end-to-end timing run's 100-share probes on pre-match books are not capacity evidence; they were bound by the net cap on deep, quiet books.
