\begin{csabstract}
In-play tennis prediction markets are priced by whoever learns the point first. We measure who that is, what they earn
and what each second is worth, from public trade tapes of 13,084 Polymarket tennis matches. A walk-forward fast tier of wallets trading within 3\,s of a score move earns
+0.93¢ per share after fees in sample and +0.75¢ in its single out-of-sample run; other
takers lose. At its own fills a frozen book earns +0.60¢ on a burned (non-blind) hold-out, but not with
fees doubled. Simulated at a 1\,s licensed-feed baseline (assumed feed latency (licensed feed not purchased); parameters measured), a computer-vision (CV) trader earns
+\$4 a day on that hold-out at the pre-registered stamp lag, where each second of feed delay costs
\$42–74 a day, and +\$57 at a post hoc estimate, on trades selected on
outcomes; a replay on real books loses. Real money: none.

\vspace{2pt}\noindent\textbf{JEL:} G14, G12, C58.\enspace \textbf{Keywords:} prediction markets; latency arbitrage;
in-play betting; tennis; computer vision; market microstructure.
\end{csabstract}

% ======================================================================================== 1 Summary
\section{Summary}\label{sec:summary}
Our CV calls the point before the ball lands; we price the feed it needs.
\begin{keybox}
\item \textbf{Strategy and edge.} Call the point with CV; lift the stale resting quote inside the 1\,s
taker delay. The book reprices 1.2\,s \emph{before} the official stamp; the fast tier earns
+0.93\,/\,+0.75¢ a share after fees (IS\,/\,OOS) every month; other takers lose.
\item \textbf{Headline, burned OOS.} v2 at fast-tier fills: +0.60¢ [0.09, 1.13], Sharpe
6.7. CV at the 1\,s baseline (assumed feed latency (licensed feed not purchased); parameters measured): +\$4/day (Sharpe 0.3) at the pre-registered \(L = 2.0\)\,s,
+\$57/day (Sharpe 8.8) at the post hoc \(L = 3.14\)\,s
[2.23–3.22]; trades selected on outcomes, not ex ante.
\item \textbf{Failed; decision.} Fees \(\times\)2 OOS (−0.34¢); every blind test of a tradable book;
the replay on real books; table tennis. Go only on a measured stamp lag \(L \geq 3.0\)\,s, which pays
for the cheapest data stack. Real money: none.
\end{keybox}

% ======================================================================================== 2 Hypothesis
\section{Economic hypothesis}\label{sec:hyp}
Win probability is a known function of the score. The leverage of point \(k\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lev}
  \Delta(k) = \Pr(\text{win}\mid \text{point } k \text{ won}) - \Pr(\text{win}\mid \text{point } k \text{ lost}),
\end{equation}
follows from the exact point-level Markov chain \citep{klaassen2001}, with mean \(|\Delta(k)|\) of 5.7\%
over the 161 points of a simulated ATP best-of-three. Each point moves fair value by a known amount
at a known instant, and the news reaches traders in tiers (Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}a): in-venue tracking; the official
stamp (unmeasured, 1–3\,s later); licensed video (0.5–8\,s, vendor-stated);
public streams (\(\geq\)12.2\,s); score feeds (28.2--44.1\,s after
the book, which reprices a median 1.2\,s \emph{before} the stamp, \(n = 482\)).
Makers whose quotes are stale when a point ends are picked off by whoever learns it first: the adverse selection of
\citet{glosten1985}, the stale-quote sniping of \citet{budish2015} and \citet{aquilina2022}, the toxic arbitrage of
\citet{foucault2017}.
\begin{hypbox}
\textbf{H6.} \emph{Universe:} Polymarket ATP/WTA/Challenger moneylines. \emph{Behaviour:} wallets trading 0--3\,s
after a score move. \emph{Horizon:} 30\,s markout. \emph{Counterparty:} the resting maker who has not yet cancelled a
stale quote. \emph{Persistence:} physics and data licences decide who learns first; the 1\,s taker delay lets makers
cancel, so only news that leads the reprice by over a second pays (a barrier to entry; the cost \(D\) in
Eq.~\ref{eq:budget}). \emph{Prediction:} fast wallets chosen on past months keep a positive net markout each month and
out of sample. \emph{Falsifier:} \(\leq 0\) in over a third of months, or out of sample. \emph{Trail:} H1--H4 committed
before any result (\texttt{7232986}); H6 frozen before the OOS opened; changes in
\texttt{DEVIATIONS.md}.
\end{hypbox}
The rent tracks the structure (v2: +1.96, +1.36, +1.45,
+1.02¢ in the 3\,s/0\%, 3\,s/3\%, 1\,s/3\%, 1\,s/5\% delay/fee regimes) and is being competed away: the
fast tier grew from 4 to 131 wallets as its edge fell
0.20¢ a month (\(t = −3.98\)); five wallets carry 81\%\,/\,142\%
of v2's P\&L; the venue now buys official data and streams the ATP itself \citep{gi2026,prn2026}. In-play mispricing
is known \citep{croxson2014,brown2012,brown2014}; new here: a wallet-level map of the latency tier, a timing of every
public source, a price on each second.

% ======================================================================================== 3 Data
\section{Data and universe}\label{sec:data}
13,084 resolved ATP/WTA/Challenger singles moneylines with \(\geq\)\$5,000 volume,
Oct 8, 2025 to Oct 3, 2026, on a public order book. IS: the first 80\% of matches by start time; OOS: the last 2,617 from 2026-08-25 14:15 UTC (21.2\% of
volume; burned for v2 and CV). A blind test on 11,307 smaller markets checks survivorship.
6 tapes may be truncated, 2,889 early matches pay no fee, 2.89\%
settle 50/50 (all kept); tape stamps lag 1.98\,s. Sources: Polymarket APIs and sports websocket
\citep{pmapi}; ESPN and WTA, latency only \citep{espn,wtaapi}; Kalshi \citep{kalshiapi}; \citet{french}; OpenTTGames
\citep{voeikov2020}; BlurBall and WASB-SBDT weights \citep{gossard2026,tarashima2023}.

% ======================================================================================== 4 Methodology
\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}
\para{Fast tier and v2} A detector fires on a \(\geq\)4¢ move of the 10\,s VWAP against the prior
60\,s. Each month a wallet qualifies on past months only (\(\geq\)30 prints 0--3\,s after a
detection, \(\geq\)10 matches, \(t > 3\) on its 30\,s markout). The frozen book
v2 copies qualifying prints, sized \(\propto 1/\sqrt{q(1-q)}\), at most \$1,000 per order and
100 shares net per match, held to resolution, at the fast tier's own prices: \emph{the opportunity at
their speed, not our execution}.

\para{CV strategy} Streamed causally on held-out 120\,fps table-tennis video, our live engine calls
4 of 41 misses before contact, none wrongly (Wilson lower bound
51\%; median lead 162\,ms);
an offline evaluation with a look-ahead feature called 11 of 11 at 50\,ms (Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}b). Frame to order-ready over
WebRTC takes 54\,ms; with a simulated 1\,s feed, network and venue hold, 2,119\,ms, under
the organiser's 3\,s bar (\texttt{results/e2e}, paper). With the bounce at time zero an order wins only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
  V + \delta + D - \ell \;<\; L + R \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V \;<\; L + R - D - \delta + \ell ,
\end{equation}
where \(V\) is the feed delay, \(\delta\) the inference (20\,ms), network and gateway, \(D\) the
1\,s taker delay, \(\ell\) the call lead, \(L\) the stamp lag and \(R\) the reprice relative to the
stamp, measured on 482 live points (fills draw on the 265 whose book moved
\(\geq\)3¢); \(V\) runs 0--60\,s, 20 seeds a cell. The trade set is every historical point the market
later repriced \(\geq\)4¢: \emph{selected on outcomes, not ex ante}. \(L\) is unmeasured:
pre-registered 2.0\,s, or a post hoc estimate of 3.14\,s (95\% CI
2.23–3.22\,s; two modes, as the stamp has 1\,s resolution) that assumes the first informed prints are
courtside humans reacting in 0.25\,s (who would also queue ahead of a 1\,s-feed order). Free of \(L\), the call must land
\(\geq\)0.89\,s before the umpire's stamp; \(V = 1\)\,s at \(L = 3.14\)\,s
equals a camera 0.14\,s \emph{ahead} of the bounce at \(L = 2.0\)\,s. Its
pre-registration set no pass/fail rule; the blind test of the frozen v3 rule did, and failed (Table~\ref{tab:fail}).

\para{Costs} Each match's own taker fee,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fee}
  f(q) = r\,q\,(1-q)\ \text{per share} \;=\; 10{,}000\,r\,(1-q)\ \text{bps of notional},
\end{equation}
with \(r = 5\%\) today (250\,bps at \(q = 0.5\)): 120\,/\,179\,bps
on v2's trades (IS\,/\,OOS). Spreads are paid as traded. Stresses: ½ and 1 tick worse entry, fees \(\times\)2,
all costs \(\times\)2 (\(\approx\)+99\,bps more). \para{Validation} Walk-forward inside IS; the fee in force;
the onset look-ahead fixed (D9); every read of held-out data logged.

% ======================================================================================== 5 Results
\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
The fast tier beats other takers every month and copying it 3\,s later loses (Fig.~\ref{fig:edge}a). At its fills v2
earns in both periods (Table~\ref{tab:v2}, Fig.~\ref{fig:edge}b); out of sample the edge is gone at ½ tick
worse entry (+0.10¢) and negative with fees doubled (−0.34¢,
0/3 months positive).
\para{Sharpe above 3} We assumed a bug and found one: fixing the onset look-ahead (D9) cut IS Sharpe from
16.8 to 14.5. The rest is about 270 small, nearly
independent bets a day (same-day correlation 0.0013). The deflated Sharpe \citep{bailey2014} is
0.997 IS at \(N = 3,386\) but 0.075–0.839 OOS: 40 days cannot rule out luck.
The CV Sharpe is a seed mean over trades selected on outcomes.
\para{The 1\,s baseline} At the pre-registered lag the CV trader makes +\$15\,/\,+\$4
a day (IS\,/\,OOS; Sharpe 2.1\,/\,0.3); at the post hoc estimate
+\$94\,/\,+\$57 (Sharpe 11.9\,/\,8.8;
Table~\ref{tab:cv}, Fig.~\ref{fig:decay}). That figure is fragile: at its interval's low end it is about
+\$19\,/\,+\$8, and the same inference read per point falls to
−\$17 (break-even 0.34\,/\,0.29\,s). The live engine's own
calls barely move the cells. Two biases flatter the sweep: trades chosen on outcomes, and a reprice timed
66\,ms late. The tests free of them lose: a replay of 9 matches
recorded live on 2026-10-03 on their real books loses in 36 of 36 settings
(−0.94¢ at \(V = 1\)\,s; Fig.~\ref{fig:replay}), and an ex-ante Markov-swing filter (exploratory) in
9 of 9 cells (Table~\ref{tab:A-sel}), so the feed delay, not the selection, sinks the 1\,s trader; the
frozen v3 rule failed blind (Table~\ref{tab:fail}).

% ======================================================================================== 6 Risk
\section{Risk management}\label{sec:risk}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Limits, in code} (\texttt{engine/risk/}). \$1,000 per order, 100
shares net per match, zone 0.05–0.95: one match can lose at most \$95 plus fees. Capital
3\(\times\) peak locked (v2 \$28,302; CV \$13,800--\$29,230). Kill
switches: stale feed \(>\)2\,s, vision \(>\)1\,s, latency above its
rolling p95, manual; the \$1,000 daily stop (3.86\,\(\sigma\) of v2's
daily P\&L) is a disaster stop.
\item \textbf{CV model risk.} Wrong calls always fill (Table~\ref{tab:cv}). MISS calls trade only at confidence
\(\geq\)0.95 and positive edge after the fee (code); live use needs 73 audited correct calls in a row, and a confirmed
false call halts the day (policy). Phantom calls outside play (about 30 an hour) erase the
1\,s OOS P\&L at 2.5 (pre-registered) or 32 (post hoc) a day, so the
rally-state gate (coded, off, unevaluated) must be on. Betting video at 25--50\,fps without early calls gives
+\$8\,/\,−\$13 a day (pre-registered).
\item \textbf{De-risking, set in advance} (policy, not code; it never fired in sample, so it is untested on a decline).
Halve size if the trailing 30-day edge is below 0.3¢ (IS minimum +0.74¢);
stop at \(\leq 0\), a 5\% drawdown, or a fee or delay change. At the fast tier's decay, v2's OOS
+0.60¢ halves size in 1.5 months and stops in 3.0. The CV
book starts only on a measured \(L \geq 3.0\)\,s.
\item \textbf{Tail, venue, legal.} Fees \(\times\)2 erase v2's OOS edge; kurtosis
4.30\,/\,3.26. The UMA oracle settles (disputes: 4--6 days); collateral is pUSD,
so only working capital sits on the venue \citep{pmuma}; US persons are close-only \citep{pmgeoblock}; courtsiding
breaches ticket terms \citep{itf2026}. Factors \citep{fama1993,carhart1997}: \(\alpha\) \(t = 8.48\),
max \(|t| = 1.36\), R² 3.3\% (IS; Table~\ref{tab:A-factor}).
\end{itemize}

% ======================================================================================== 7 Liquidity
\section{Liquidity and capacity}\label{sec:liq}
v2 trades \$7,182 a day, 0.07\% of match volume; its OOS CI excludes zero
only up to 1\(\times\) size (\$22,754) and 5\(\times\) loses (Fig.~\ref{fig:cap}a). Median stale
depth before a reprice: \$222\,/\,\$379\,/\,\$564 at
0\,/\,1\,/\,2\,s; spread 1¢. The CV book (Table~\ref{tab:cv}: 10 matches a day, half the stale
depth) at the post hoc estimate halves its Sharpe at \$41,000 of capital OOS
(\$66/day) and \$74,000 IS (\$181/day), or
\$96,000\,/\,\$164,000 covering every match; at the pre-registered lag its Sharpe
is 2.7\,/\,0.9 at the smallest size: no capacity. Its notional is
0.15\% of in-play volume but 6\% of the fast tier's 0--3\,s volume: the
seconds after each point bind. Fixed costs decide: the book can pay at most \$1,644 a month for
data OOS (post hoc) or \$55 (pre-registered), against an assumed licence of
\$1,250--\$10,000; the central \$167-a-day stack is covered at no
\(L \leq 3.0\)\,s, the cheapest (\$42) only at \(L \geq\) 2.87–2.98\,s.
Covering every match, the camera-free stack (\$242--\$256 a day) breaks
even from about \$52,000 (IS) and \$99,000 (OOS) of capital (\texttt{results/capacity}); v2's
OOS break-even fee is 8.2\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:cap}b).

% ======================================================================================== 8 Limitations
\section{Limitations and next steps}\label{sec:limits}
We hold a cheap real option on one number, the stamp lag \(L\), which moves the break-even one for one
(Eq.~\ref{eq:budget}). A live session with a licensed low-latency feed and small test orders measures it, and whether
delayed orders are released on a 1\,s clock (35\% of reprices land just after a whole second); go if
\(L \geq 3.0\)\,s. Licensing turns the simulation into deployment (Polymarket itself buys official data;
\citealp{gi2026}); a faster feed moves left on the curve (Fig.~\ref{fig:decay}a). CV accuracy is from table tennis
(illiquid: spread 94¢); tennis is simulated (Fig.~\ref{fig:spin}). Forward test: pending (runs once, Oct 4);
live paper session: pending (paper; 0 fills so far).\label{lastmain}
