\begin{csabstract}
After each point, a Polymarket tennis price (a win probability) is set by whoever learns the point first. In public
data on 13,084 matches, wallets that repeatedly trade within 3\,s of a point earn after fees in all
11 months, in and out of sample, while everyone else loses; copying them has no factor exposure
and an out-of-sample Sharpe ratio of 6.7 that doubled fees erase. About half the edge is gone within
1--2\,s, and the first second of video delay costs a simulated computer-vision (CV) trader \$42–74 a
day; our pipeline needs 54\,ms. With an assumed feed delay (no feed purchased), that trader makes,
pre-registered, +\$15, +\$4 and −\$13 a day out of sample at 0.5, 1 and 3\,s
(+\$81, +\$57, −\$11 post hoc). Every blind test of a book we could trade
failed.

\vspace{3pt}\noindent\textbf{JEL:} G14, G12, C58.\enspace \textbf{Keywords:} prediction markets; latency arbitrage;
in-play betting; tennis.
\end{csabstract}

% ======================================================================================== 1 Summary
\section{Summary}\label{sec:summary}
\begin{keybox}
\item Each tennis point changes who is likely to win, and on Polymarket the price \emph{is} that probability. Quotes
lag the point by about a second, so whoever learns it first sets the price: the wallets that repeatedly trade in the first 3\,s after a point (the \emph{fast tier}, picked on past months)
earn after fees in all 11 months, in and out of sample, and
everyone else loses. Copying them has no factor exposure and an out-of-sample Sharpe ratio of 6.7,
which doubled fees wipe out.
\item About half the edge is gone 1--2\,s after the point (+1.18¢ a share in the block of the
move, +0.59¢ at 1--2\,s, in sample), and the first second of feed delay costs a simulated CV trader \$42–74 a day. Our
pipeline adds 54\,ms: with a 1\,s feed an order can execute 2,119\,ms after the point,
inside the 3\,s bar.
\item With an assumed feed delay, the pre-registered simulation makes +\$15,
+\$4 and −\$13 a day out of sample at 0.5, 1 and 3\,s, so it pays only
with a feed near 1\,s or faster (post hoc: +\$81, +\$57 and
−\$11).
\item Every blind test of a book we could actually trade failed, and so did a replay on real order books; we report
all 4,219 variants and 77 reads of held-out data, and used no real money.
\end{keybox}

% ======================================================================================== 2 Hypothesis
\section{Economic hypothesis}\label{sec:hyp}
Given how often each player wins a point on serve, a Markov model of points, games and sets gives the exact chance that each player wins from any score \citep{klaassen2001}. A Polymarket price is
that chance in cents, so each point moves fair value by a known amount at a known moment. We call that amount the
\emph{swing} \(\Delta(k)\) of point \(k\): the chance of winning the match if point \(k\) is won, minus the chance if
it is lost. It averages 5.7\% and reaches 38.4\% on a big point (Appendix~A).

The news reaches traders in tiers: the court first, then video, then public score feeds
28.2--44.1\,s after the price has moved (Fig.~\ref{fig:race}). A maker whose quote still rests after the point is picked off by whoever knows first: the adverse selection of \citet{glosten1985} and the
latency race \citet{budish2015} and \citet{aquilina2022} measured in stocks. In-play betting prices react late \citep{croxson2014,brown2012}. We add a wallet-level map of who is fast and a price for each
second. Polymarket holds every \emph{taker} order (one that hits a resting quote) for
1\,s, so makers can cancel. The edge is shrinking: the fast tier grew from 4 to 131 wallets
as its edge fell 0.20¢ a month (\(t = −3.98\)).

\begin{hypbox}
\textbf{H6 (pre-registered).} We expect wallets that trade within 3\,s of a score move in Polymarket ATP, WTA and
Challenger match markets to earn a positive net \emph{markout} over 30\,s (profit at the price 30\,s later, after
fees), because the other side is a maker who has not yet cancelled a stale quote. The edge persists because physics
and data licences decide who learns first. If so, fast wallets picked on past months only stay positive every month
and out of sample; H6 fails if the markout is \(\leq 0\) in over a third of months, or out of sample. H1--H4 were
committed before any result (\texttt{7232986}, 2026-10-03 09:50 UTC); H6 was frozen before
we opened the held-out data.
\end{hypbox}

% ======================================================================================== 3 Data
\section{Data and universe}\label{sec:data}
We use every resolved ATP, WTA and Challenger singles match market on Polymarket with at least
\$5,000 traded, Oct 8, 2025 to Oct 3, 2026: 13,084 matches and
\$2.84B of volume, from public order books and trade tapes \citep{pmapi}. \emph{In sample} (IS) is the
first 80\% of matches by start time; \emph{out of sample} (OOS) is the last 2,617, from
2026-08-25 14:15 UTC (the last 10.7\% by time). We looked at the OOS while building v2 and the
CV simulation, so it is \emph{burned}. For a blind test, which also checks survivorship, we held back
11,307 smaller markets we never examined and opened them once per frozen rule. Six tapes hit the API's
offset cap, 2,889 early matches paid no fee, 2.89\% of markets settled 50/50 (kept),
tape timestamps run 1.98\,s late, and no corporate actions apply. Timing data: ESPN and the WTA
\citep{espn,wtaapi}; vision: OpenTTGames footage \citep{voeikov2020}, BlurBall, WASB, TrackNet and
court-keypoint weights \citep{gossard2026,tarashima2023,huang2019,yastrebksv} and one Pexels tennis rally
\citep{pexels2021}.

% ======================================================================================== 4 Methodology
\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}
\para{The fast tier and v2} We flag a score move when the 10\,s volume-weighted price moves at least
4¢ away from its prior 60\,s. Each month a wallet joins the fast tier if, in earlier months, it
traded at least 30 times 0--3\,s after a flag, in at least 10
matches, with \(t > 3\). Our copy book, \emph{v2}, copies their trades, sizes each by its
resolution risk (the best of 55 sizing rules, picked walk-forward), caps orders at \$1,000 and exposure at 100 shares net per match,
and holds to resolution. At their own prices it measures what their speed is worth, not what we could earn: copying the same trades 3\,s later loses (Fig.~\ref{fig:edge}a).

\para{Being fast ourselves} Our second strategy uses computer vision (CV): a model watches the video, calls the point as
early as it can and sends a taker order before the stale quote is cancelled. We have
no video of the matches we trade, so we simulate this trader on past points with an assumed feed latency (licensed feed not purchased); all other parameters
are measured. The order wins only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
  V + \delta + D - \ell \;<\; L + R \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V \;<\; L + R - D - \delta + \ell ,
\end{equation}
where \(V\) is the feed delay (how late the video reaches us), \(\delta\) our pipeline plus network time, \(D\) the
1\,s taker hold, \(\ell\) how early the CV calls the point, \(L\) the \emph{stamp lag} (how long
after the ball lands the umpire's official point stamp is logged) and \(R\) when the book reprices relative to that
stamp. On 482 points the book moved 1.2\,s before the stamp. Nobody
publishes \(L\), so we show two readings: 2.0\,s, which we \emph{pre-registered} (wrote down before
seeing any result; \texttt{PREREG.md}, 17:25 UTC), and 3.14\,s, a \emph{post
hoc} estimate from one day of data. The simulated trades are past points where the price later moved at least
4¢, so they are \emph{selected on outcomes, not ex ante}.

Our CV is real on table tennis. On 41 held-out balls that went out (120\,fps), our live causal engine called
4 early (median lead 162\,ms) and none wrongly, at 119.9\,fps on
one L4 GPU. On a freely licensed real tennis rally \citep{pexels2021}, our tennis tracker found the ball in
89\% of 300 frames (48/48 random spot-checks on the ball in
play) and fitted the court lines in every frame, a median 0.36\,px off the paint; we make no
in/out calls on single-camera tennis footage. On our own footage a frame becomes a built order in 54\,ms, and with a simulated 1\,s
feed, 65\,ms of network and the 1\,s hold the order can execute 2,119\,ms after the
point, inside the organizers' 3\,s bar (Fig.~\ref{fig:f2t}).

\end{figure}

\para{Costs and validation} Polymarket charges takers \(f(q) = r\,q\,(1-q)\) a share at price \(q\), which is
\(10{,}000\,r\,(1-q)\) bps of notional. Today \(r = 5\%\): a trade at 50¢ pays
1.25¢ a share. Each match pays its own fee, every trade the spread it crossed; we also double both. v2 paid 120 and
179\,bps in fees (IS, OOS) and still netted 273 and
114\,bps. We tune only in sample, walk-forward (fit on past months, test on the next).

\para{A Sharpe ratio of 14.5 is a red flag} We assumed a bug and found one: the detector's start

\para{What speed is worth} Pre-registered, the CV trader breaks even at a 1.01\,s feed delay
out of sample (Table~\ref{tab:lat}, Fig.~\ref{fig:speed}): the call must reach the venue
0.89\,s before the umpire's stamp. Post hoc it makes +\$57 a day at
1\,s (+\$8 at the low end of the stamp-lag interval), but read per point the post hoc inference
loses (−\$17 a day). Every reading loses at 3\,s, so meeting the 3\,s bar is not enough. A replay
of 9 matches recorded live on Oct~3 against their real books, calling every point ex ante, loses in
36 of 36 settings (−0.94¢ a share at 1\,s;
Fig.~\ref{fig:replay}): the gap is knowing which points matter in advance.

\para{What failed} Every blind test of a book we could trade failed: v2 on the 11,307 unseen markets
(+1.22¢ [−0.19, 2.65]), the pre-registered maker book and the CV rule v3
(−0.52¢); Appendix~B lists every test. We tried 4,219 variants, read held-out data
77 times, changed rules after a look 2 times for v2 and
13 for the CV simulation, and chose the post hoc reading and 1\,s base case after looking.
Blind forward test: pre-registered but not run within the hackathon window (HYPOTHESIS\_V2.md A5).

% ======================================================================================== 6 Risk
\section{Risk management}\label{sec:risk}
\para{Limits and de-risking} Each order is at most \$1,000 and each match at most
100 shares net, at prices from 5¢ to 95¢, so one match can lose at most \$95 plus
fees; a \$1,000 daily loss ends the day, all in code. Set in advance, we stop if the
score feed is over 2\,s stale, vision over 1\,s stale, or latency
above its rolling 95th percentile; we halve size if the trailing 30-day edge falls below 0.3¢,
and stop if it reaches zero, if drawdown hits 5\%, or if the venue changes its fee or hold.

\para{Model, concentration and tail risk} Wrong CV calls always fill, so an out call trades only at confidence
\(\geq\)0.95 and one confirmed false call halts the day. Phantom calls outside play come about
30 an hour; 2.5 a day would erase the pre-registered 1\,s OOS P\&L
(32 post hoc). Replayed on the engine's held-out call log, our rally gate (trade a miss call
only within 2.0\,s of a bounce call, set before the test) removes 5 of the 7 calls on unlabelled balls (4 of the
5 between rallies) but keeps only 3 of
5 correct calls (2 at 0.5–0.8\,s), leaving
about 8 phantom calls an hour: not yet safe to trade. The top five copied wallets
carry 81\% of v2's P\&L in sample and 142\% out of sample. The UMA oracle settles
markets \citep{pmuma}, US persons may only close positions \citep{pmgeoblock}, and courtsiding breaches ticket terms
\citep{itf2026}. Factor risk is nil (largest factor
\(|t| = 1.36\); \(\alpha\) has \(t = 8.48\)) \citep{fama1993,carhart1997}.

% ======================================================================================== 7 Liquidity
\section{Liquidity and capacity}\label{sec:liq}
v2 trades \$7,182 a day, 0.07\% of match volume; its OOS confidence interval
stays above zero only up to 1\(\times\) size (\$22,754 of capital), and 5\(\times\) loses. The
CV book has no capacity at the pre-registered lag (Sharpe 2.7 IS, 0.8
OOS even at its smallest size). Post hoc, taking at most half the stale depth at each point, its Sharpe halves at
\$40,000 of capital OOS and \$73,000 IS and peaks at \$177 a
day on \$83,000 OOS (Fig.~\ref{fig:capacity}). What binds is the few seconds after each point: the
book is 0.22\% of in-play volume but 9\% of what the fast tier trades
0--3\,s after a point. Data is the other limit: at 1\,s the book could pay at most
\$1,644 a month for data post hoc and \$55 pre-registered, against
reported feed prices of \$1,250--\$10,000. At today's fee COURTSIDE prices speed; it
is not yet a business.

% ======================================================================================== 8 Limitations
\section{Limitations and next steps}\label{sec:limits}
The stamp lag \(L\) is unmeasured and moves the break-even one for one (Eq.~\ref{eq:budget}); the 1\,s hold
dominates it, since even for a 20\,ms bot the inferred lag is 2.85\,s. One session with a licensed feed
and a few test orders would measure \(L\); at 1\,s the feed pays for itself only if \(L \geq 3.0\)\,s, so
buying it is a business decision we have not made. Our CV call accuracy comes from table tennis, the trading is
simulated and we never sent an order. A fee rise, a shorter hold or more fast wallets would break the strategy.\label{lastmain}

\csappendix
\section*{Appendix A\quad Calculations}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Appendix}
Everything needed for scoring is in the main pages. Below: formulas with worked examples (A), every test (B), extra
figures (C) and the records (D). Each value is in \texttt{results/paper/numbers.json} with its source file.

\para{A1. Sharpe ratio} \(\text{SR} = \sqrt{365}\,\bar d / s_d\), where \(d_t\) is net P\&L on every calendar day
(zero on days without trades) and \(s_d\) its standard deviation. v2 IS: \(\bar d = \$196.24\),
\(s_d = \$258.87\) over \(T = 206\) days, so the daily ratio is 0.758
and \(\text{SR} = 0.758 \times 19.105 = 14.48\). OOS:
\(\$92.19 / \$264.15 \times 19.105 = 6.67\).

\para{A2. Sortino ratio} \(\sqrt{365}\,\bar d / \text{DD}\), where the downside deviation is \(\text{DD} =
\sqrt{\tfrac1T\sum_t \min(d_t,0)^2}\). For v2 OOS, \(\text{DD} = \$125.64\), so the ratio is
\(\sqrt{365}\times \$92.19 / \$125.64 = 14.0\).

\para{A3. Maximum drawdown} \(\text{MDD} = \min_t \big(E_t - \max_{s\le t} E_s\big)\), where \(E_t\) is cumulative
P\&L, as a share of capital. v2 OOS: \(−\$469 / \$22,754 = −2.06\%\).

\para{A4. Probabilistic Sharpe} The chance that the true daily Sharpe beats \(\text{SR}^*\), given the sample length
\(T\), skew \(\gamma_3\) and kurtosis \(\gamma_4\) \citep{bailey2014}:
\[\text{PSR}(\text{SR}^*) = \Phi\Big((\widehat{\text{SR}}-\text{SR}^*)\sqrt{T-1}\,\Big/\sqrt{1-\gamma_3\widehat{\text{SR}}+\tfrac{\gamma_4-1}{4}\widehat{\text{SR}}^2}\Big).\]
v2 OOS: \(\widehat{\text{SR}} = 0.349\), \(T = 40\),
\(\gamma_3 = 0.326\), \(\gamma_4 = 3.26\), so \(\text{PSR}(0) = 0.987\).

\para{A5. Deflated Sharpe} \(\text{DSR} = \text{PSR}(\text{SR}_0)\), where \(\text{SR}_0\) is the best Sharpe we would
expect from \(N\) tries on pure noise, with \(\gamma\) the Euler--Mascheroni constant:
\[\text{SR}_0 = \sqrt{V}\Big((1-\gamma)\,\Phi^{-1}\big(1-\tfrac1N\big) + \gamma\,\Phi^{-1}\big(1-\tfrac{1}{Ne}\big)\Big).\] v2 OOS with \(N =
3,386\) and \(V = 1/(T-1)\): the two \(\Phi^{-1}\) terms are 3.436 and 3.698,
\(\text{SR}_0 = 0.574\) a day (10.97 a year), and DSR \(= 0.075\).
Table~\ref{tab:head} shows the lowest DSR over three choices of \(V\).

\para{A6. Probability of backtest overfitting} \citep{bailey2017}. Split the daily P\&L of 24
variants into \(S = 16\) blocks. For each of the \(\binom{16}{8} = 12,870\) ways
to call half the blocks in sample, take the best in-sample variant, find its rank \(\omega\) out of sample and set
\(\lambda = \ln\frac{\omega}{1-\omega}\); PBO \(= \Pr(\lambda \le 0)\). v2-safe grid: PBO \(= 15\%\)
(median \(\lambda = 1.27\)).

\para{A7. Stationary block bootstrap} \citep{politis1994}. Resample days in blocks of random length (mean 5 days)
10,000 times and read the percentiles. v2 OOS Sharpe: [1.9, 12.3], standard error 2.65,
\(P(\text{SR}\le 0) = 0.0023\).

\para{A8. Factor regression} \(r_t = \alpha + \beta_M\,\text{MktRF}_t + \beta_S\,\text{SMB}_t + \beta_H\,\text{HML}_t
+ \beta_U\,\text{Mom}_t + \varepsilon_t\) on daily return on capital, with Newey--West errors (5 lags)
\citep{fama1993,carhart1997,newey1987,french}. v2 IS (138 days): \(\alpha = 0.801\%\) a day
(\(t = 8.48\)); \(\beta_M = 0.173\) (\(t = 1.36\)),
\(\beta_S = 0.085\), \(\beta_H = 0.044\), \(\beta_U = 0.028\);
\(R^2 = 3.3\%\).

\para{A9. Taker fee} \(f(q) = r\,q(1-q)\) a share at price \(q\), or \(r(1-q)\) per dollar, which is
\(10{,}000\,r(1-q)\) bps. At \(r = 5\%\): \(q = 0.5\) pays 1.25¢
(250\,bps) and \(q = 0.8\) pays 0.80¢ (100\,bps).

\para{A10. Point swing} \(\Delta(k)\) of Section~\ref{sec:hyp}, with the match-win probability computed exactly by recursion over points,
games, tiebreaks and sets (\texttt{src/markov.py}). Two equal players who win 0.645 of service points:
at the first point \(\Pr(\text{win}) = 50.0\%\), moving to 51.2\% or
47.8\%, so \(\Delta = 3.4\%\). Serving at 5--5, 30--40 in the deciding
set, \(\Pr(\text{win}) = 33.7\%\), moving to 47.3\% or 8.9\%, so
\(\Delta = 38.4\%\). The mean \(|\Delta|\) over a match is 5.7\%.

\para{A11. Latency budget} Eq.~\ref{eq:budget}. With the pre-registered \(L = 2.0\)\,s, \(D =
1\)\,s and our measured \(\delta = 0.119\)\,s, a point whose book reprices at the
median \(R = −1.16\)\,s needs \(V < −0.28 + \ell\): no feed wins it
without an early call. At the 90th percentile, \(R = 0.08\)\,s and \(V < 0.96\)\,s.
The sweep's break-even (1.09\,s IS) comes from these late reprices.

\para{A12. Capital and return on capital} Capital is three times the peak dollars locked in open positions,
each held 4\,h or until resolution. v2 IS: peak \$9,434, capital \$28,302; return \(= 365\,\bar
d/\text{capital} = 365 \times \$196.24 / \$28,302 = 253\%\) a year.

\para{A13. Capacity and volume share} Capacity is the capital at which the seed-mean Sharpe falls to half its
smallest-size value; share is our notional a day over market volume a day. CV, post hoc, IS, just below half Sharpe:
\$10,428 a day against \$4.71M of in-play volume (0.22\%) and
\$117,047 traded by the fast tier 0--3\,s after a point (9\%).
