\begin{csabstract}
We built a predictive ball-tracking engine that calls a point (ball out, or into the net) before the ball lands: on
held-out table tennis it called 4 of 41 misses a median
162\,ms early, none wrong, at 119.9\,fps, and on broadcast tennis its out calls were
right 5 of 5 at the bounce and 3 of 3 when 33\,ms ahead. On
13,084 Polymarket ATP/WTA matches, wallets that trade within 3\,s of a point earn after fees in all
11 months. Copying them at their own fills (v2) has a Sharpe ratio of 14.5 in sample
and 6.7 out of sample (many small, nearly independent bets; Section~\ref{sec:summary});
doubled fees erase it out of sample. A simulated computer-vision (CV) trader with an assumed 1\,s feed makes
+\$4 a day out of sample pre-registered and +\$57 (Sharpe
8.8) post hoc; with a licensed 0.5\,s feed, +\$15 or
+\$81, and on 16 newer matches pre-registered before we fetched them,
−\$20.5 or +\$50.8 (anecdotal). Doubled fees and every blind test of a
tradable book failed.

\vspace{2pt}\noindent\textbf{JEL:} G14, G12, C58.\enspace \textbf{Keywords:} computer vision; ball tracking; prediction
markets; latency arbitrage; in-play betting; tennis.
\end{csabstract}

% ======================================================================================== 1 Summary
\section{Introduction and summary}\label{sec:summary}
A camera films the court; a model finds the ball, fits its flight and calls the point before the bounce
(Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}). A Polymarket price \emph{is} the chance that a player wins and lags each point by about a
second, so whoever learns the point first sets the price. We map who is fast, price each second of feed delay
(\$42–74 a day for the first) and test it on a pre-registered fresh holdout.

\para{Our Sharpe ratios are above 3, so we hunted for bugs} v2's 14.5 comes from about
270 small bets a day on points already played, held to resolution, with a mean same-day
correlation of 0.0013, so daily P\&L is rarely negative; the CV trader is the same kind of event
book. We found one bug: the score-move detector's start time used later prices (D9); the fix cut the in-sample Sharpe
from 16.8 to 14.5. Then: at zero feed delay the latency sweep reproduces the
published CV result exactly (the \(V = 0\) identity); copying the same trades 3\,s later loses (−1.05¢
a share IS), as does a copier filled after the 1.98\,s block lag and the venue's hold
(−0.47¢ IS, −1.37¢ OOS); our causal engine, with no look-ahead feature, nearly matches
Table~\ref{tab:head}'s 1\,s cells; and 62 of 62 paper numbers re-derive in SQL (20 recomputed from rows,
42 looked up).

% ======================================================================================== 2 Hypothesis
\section{Economic hypothesis}\label{sec:hyp}
A Markov model of points, games and sets gives the exact chance that each player wins from any score
\citep{klaassen2001}, so each point moves fair value by a known amount at a known moment (the \emph{swing}: mean
5.7\%, 38.4\% on a big point). The news reaches traders in tiers: court, video, then
score feeds 28.2--44.1\,s after the price moved. A maker whose quote
still rests after the point is picked off by whoever knows first \citep{glosten1985,budish2015,aquilina2022}, as
courtsiders do in betting markets \citep{croxson2014,brown2012}: on 482 live points a median
\$222 of quotes still rested at stale prices 0.25\,s before the reprice
(\$0 after 0.5\,s), and Polymarket holds each taker order 1\,s.

\begin{hypbox}
\textbf{H6 (pre-registered).} We expect wallets that trade within 3\,s of a score move in Polymarket ATP and WTA match
markets to earn a positive net \emph{markout} over 30\,s (profit at the price 30\,s later, after fees), because the
other side is a maker who has not yet cancelled a stale quote; the edge persists because physics and data licences
decide who learns first. Wallets picked on past months then stay positive every month and out of sample; H6 fails if
the markout is \(\leq 0\) in over a third of months, or out of sample. H1--H4 were committed before any result
(\texttt{7232986}); H6, written after the in-sample tier results (D5), was frozen before any
held-out read.
\end{hypbox}

\textbf{Will the edge last?}\enspace The fast tier's net edge fell 0.16¢ a month (CI
0.06–0.25), mostly as the fee rose; before fees there is no clear trend (−0.02¢,
CI [−0.12, 0.07]). A line hits zero 3.3 months after September (CI
0.6–12.8), but a May step (picked by the data) fits as well with no trend: we cannot tell if the edge is
dying, though a fee above 8.4\% (today 5\%) would end it, and entrants split the pool
(one wallet's monthly share fell from \$1,632 to \$387).

% ======================================================================================== 3 Data
\section{Data and universe}\label{sec:data}
Every resolved ATP/WTA singles match market on Polymarket with at least \$5,000 traded:
13,084 matches (1,310 at Grand Slams; \$2.84B), Oct 2025--Oct 2026,
from the public APIs \citep{pmapi}, point in time, so nothing is dropped for how it ended: 378
settled 50/50 (302 never started) and 283 ended in a retirement, all at their
settlement price. \emph{In sample} (IS) is the first 80\% of matches by start time, \emph{out of sample} (OOS) the last
2,617, from 2026-08-25 14:15 UTC (the 20\%-or-two-years rule); we looked at it while building v2 and
the CV simulation, so it is \emph{burned}. Blind tests used 11,307 held-back markets and
16 newer matches; vision and point data are in Table~\ref{tab:data}
\citep{huang2019,voeikov2020,sackmann2026}.

\para{Data problems} Six tapes hit the API's row cap (kept); 2,889 early matches had no published fee
schedule (charged none); tape times are block times 1.98\,s late, so signals use only prints
stamped before them; gaps are carried forward, never filled from later prints (the CV trade set, selected on outcomes,
is the labelled exception); no corporate actions apply. Regimes: the taker hold fell from 3\,s to 1\,s on
May 15 and the fee rose from 3\% to 5\%; v2 is positive in each (+1.96¢ to
+1.02¢ IS), and the CV trader is simulated only on the 7,755 matches with the 1\,s hold.

% ======================================================================================== 4 Methodology
\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}
\para{The fast tier and v2} Each month a wallet joins the fast tier if, in earlier months, it traded at least
30 times 0--3\,s after a score move (a \(\geq\)4¢ move of the 10\,s price
from its prior 60\,s) in 10+ matches (\(t > 3\)).
v2 copies their trades, sized by resolution risk (best of 55 rules, walk-forward), with
\$1,000 orders and 100 shares net per match, held to resolution. Copying is not
new; we add picking wallets by speed relative to the point, the sizing and limits, and the test that a copier loses:
v2 prices speed and is not our trade.

\para{Being fast ourselves} A CV model watches the video, calls the point early and sends a taker order before the stale
quote is cancelled. Without video of the matches we trade, we simulate it on past points with an assumed feed latency
(licensed feed not purchased); all other parameters are measured. The order wins only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
  V + \delta + D - \ell \;<\; L + R \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V \;<\; L + R - D - \delta + \ell ,
\end{equation}
with \(V\) the feed delay, \(\delta\) our pipeline plus network, \(D\) the 1\,s hold, \(\ell\) how
early the CV calls, \(L\) the \emph{stamp lag} (landing to official stamp) and \(R\) the reprice relative to the stamp
(median −1.16\,s). Nobody publishes \(L\): we \emph{pre-registered}
2.0\,s and also show 3.14\,s, a \emph{post hoc} estimate from one day. The trades
are past points where the price later moved \(\geq\)4¢: \emph{selected on outcomes, not ex ante}.

\para{Vision and speed} On held-out TrackNet broadcast games one TV camera's out calls were right
5 of 5, 3 of 3 and 3 of 4 at 0, 33 and 67\,ms ahead, but only bounce-time
calls met our 95\% rule. Our live engine makes about 30 phantom calls an hour outside play
(8 with a rally gate): not yet safe to trade. With a simulated 1\,s feed, our 54\,ms
pipeline, 65\,ms of network and the hold, an order can execute 2,119\,ms after the point
(p99 2,218\,ms; built, not sent), inside the 3\,s bar. A teammate's London server saw the book first on
99.74\% of 287,577 messages (clock-corrected); with measured cold REST round trips
(70\,ms London, 184\,ms Florida) the order executes in
2,089--2,146\,ms (2,512\,ms at worst; not order latency).

\para{Costs and validation} Each match charges its own taker fee \(f(q) = r\,q(1-q)\) a share, \(10{,}000\,r(1-q)\) bps
of notional \citep[fee schedule]{pmapi}; today \(r = 5\%\), 250\,bps at 50¢.
Fees paid were 120 and 179\,bps of notional for v2 (IS, OOS) and
163–226\,bps for the CV trader; v2's real fills include the spread crossed, and the CV fill walks
the measured stale book. The stress doubles the fee (v2: also the spread). We tune only in sample, walk-forward.

% ======================================================================================== 6 Risk
\section{Risk management}\label{sec:risk}
\para{Limits and the most we can lose} Orders are at most \$1,000 and a match at most
100 shares net at 5--95¢, which caps the open position at \$95, not the loss: v2
buys both tokens in 79\% of matches IS (71\% OOS), and the worst
match made −\$206 IS, −\$154 OOS. Only the backtest's \$3,000 gross cap per
match bounds a match, at \$1,479; the live engine lacks it. A \$1,000 daily loss ends
the day. Set in advance, we stop on a stale feed or vision, or slow latency, halve size if the trailing 30-day edge falls
below 0.3¢, and stop at zero, at a 5\% drawdown or a fee or hold change. None
fired in sample.

\para{Model and concentration} Wrong CV calls always fill, so out calls need confidence \(\geq\)0.95 and one false call
halts the day. The top five copied wallets carry 81\% of v2's P\&L IS and 142\%
OOS. A per-wallet limit (\$1,000 a day, retiring wallets whose trailing edge is \(\leq 0\); backtest only),
pre-registered on IS, kept 63\% of IS P\&L and doubled its drawdown; once on the burned OOS it made
about the same (\$3,615 vs \$3,688; Sharpe 7.6 vs
6.7, CIs overlap), with a better worst day but worse drawdown, and the top five still at
129\% (the rest lose at the same rate): a loss limit, not a fix. Settlement,
US access and ticket terms add risk \citep{pmuma,pmgeoblock,itf2026}; factor risk is nil (largest
\(|t| = 1.36\)).

% ======================================================================================== 7 Liquidity
\section{Liquidity and capacity}\label{sec:liq}
v2 holds its OOS edge up to \$22,754 of capital (5\(\times\) loses), trading
0.07\% of match volume; the CV book's Sharpe halves at
\$40,000--\$73,000 post hoc (Fig.~\ref{fig:capacity}), and pre-registered it has no
capacity. Price impact, fitted on 4.5 million IS taker orders with a usable mid, grows with the log of
size (0.75¢ a share at 1--10 shares, 1.17¢ at 10,000+ in the
highest-volume fifth), the mid moves 0.05–0.14¢ more per 1,000 shares at 30\,s (less on burned-OOS
tapes: the fit is unstable), and in four hours of live book, buying just after a reprice cost 1.71 times a
quiet book. With that charged, v2's Sharpe still halves near \$28.0k OOS, but 1\(\times\)
earns \$88 a day, not \$92 (\$71
conservatively, interval [−\$7, \$150]); CV capacity moves under 1\%.
Taking half the stale depth is cautious: in 907 live reprices and 265
stamped points, other takers took 4–11\% of it. COURTSIDE prices speed; it is not yet a business.

% ======================================================================================== 8 Limitations
\section{Limitations and next steps}\label{sec:limits}
The stamp lag \(L\) is unmeasured and moves the break-even one for one; trading is simulated, the vision is tested on
other matches and the fresh holdout is two days. A fee rise, a shorter hold or more fast wallets would break the edge.
Next: a week of licensed feed to measure \(L\), the forward test, and the limits in the live engine.\label{lastmain}

\csappendix
\section*{Appendix A\quad Calculations}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Appendix}
Everything needed for scoring is in the main pages. Below: formulas with worked examples (A), every test (B), extra
figures (C) and the records (D). Each value is in \texttt{results/paper/numbers.json} with its source file.

\para{A1. Sharpe ratio} \(\text{SR} = \sqrt{365}\,\bar d / s_d\), where \(d_t\) is net P\&L on every calendar day
(zero on days without trades) and \(s_d\) its standard deviation. v2 IS: \(\bar d = \$196.24\),
\(s_d = \$258.87\) over \(T = 206\) days, so the daily ratio is 0.758
and \(\text{SR} = 0.758 \times 19.105 = 14.48\). OOS:
\(\$92.19 / \$264.15 \times 19.105 = 6.67\).

\para{A2. Sortino ratio} \(\sqrt{365}\,\bar d / \text{DD}\), where the downside deviation is \(\text{DD} =
\sqrt{\tfrac1T\sum_t \min(d_t,0)^2}\). For v2 OOS, \(\text{DD} = \$125.64\), so the ratio is
\(\sqrt{365}\times \$92.19 / \$125.64 = 14.0\).

\para{A3. Maximum drawdown} \(\text{MDD} = \min_t \big(E_t - \max_{s\le t} E_s\big)\), where \(E_t\) is cumulative
P\&L, as a share of capital. v2 OOS: \(−\$469 / \$22,754 = −2.06\%\).

\para{A4. Probabilistic Sharpe} The chance that the true daily Sharpe beats \(\text{SR}^*\), given the sample length
\(T\), skew \(\gamma_3\) and kurtosis \(\gamma_4\) \citep{bailey2014}:
\[\text{PSR}(\text{SR}^*) = \Phi\Big((\widehat{\text{SR}}-\text{SR}^*)\sqrt{T-1}\,\Big/\sqrt{1-\gamma_3\widehat{\text{SR}}+\tfrac{\gamma_4-1}{4}\widehat{\text{SR}}^2}\Big).\]
v2 OOS: \(\widehat{\text{SR}} = 0.349\), \(T = 40\),
\(\gamma_3 = 0.326\), \(\gamma_4 = 3.26\), so \(\text{PSR}(0) = 0.987\).

\para{A5. Deflated Sharpe} \(\text{DSR} = \text{PSR}(\text{SR}_0)\), where \(\text{SR}_0\) is the best Sharpe we would
expect from \(N\) tries on pure noise, with \(\gamma\) the Euler--Mascheroni constant:
\[\text{SR}_0 = \sqrt{V}\Big((1-\gamma)\,\Phi^{-1}\big(1-\tfrac1N\big) + \gamma\,\Phi^{-1}\big(1-\tfrac{1}{Ne}\big)\Big).\] v2 OOS with \(N =
3,386\) and \(V = 1/(T-1)\): the two \(\Phi^{-1}\) terms are 3.436 and 3.698,
\(\text{SR}_0 = 0.574\) a day (10.97 a year), and DSR \(= 0.075\).
Table~\ref{tab:head} shows the lowest DSR over three choices of \(V\). \(N = 3,386\) is our variant
count when the deflation ran; the count is now 4,219, which the haircut of Section~\ref{sec:results}
uses (at that \(N\) the OOS haircut is already 0).

\para{A6. Probability of backtest overfitting} \citep{bailey2017}. Split the daily P\&L of 24
variants into \(S = 16\) blocks. For each of the \(\binom{16}{8} = 12,870\) ways
to call half the blocks in sample, take the best in-sample variant, find its rank \(\omega\) out of sample and set
\(\lambda = \ln\frac{\omega}{1-\omega}\); PBO \(= \Pr(\lambda \le 0)\). v2-safe grid: PBO \(= 15\%\)
(median \(\lambda = 1.27\)).

\para{A7. Stationary block bootstrap} \citep{politis1994}. Resample days in blocks of random length (mean 5 days)
10,000 times and read the percentiles. v2 OOS Sharpe: [1.9, 12.3], standard error 2.65,
\(P(\text{SR}\le 0) = 0.0023\).

\para{A8. Factor regression} \(r_t = \alpha + \beta_M\,\text{MktRF}_t + \beta_S\,\text{SMB}_t + \beta_H\,\text{HML}_t
+ \beta_U\,\text{Mom}_t + \varepsilon_t\) on daily return on capital, with Newey--West errors (5 lags)
\citep{fama1993,carhart1997,newey1987,french}. v2 IS (138 days): \(\alpha = 0.801\%\) a day
(\(t = 8.48\)); \(\beta_M = 0.173\) (\(t = 1.36\)),
\(\beta_S = 0.085\), \(\beta_H = 0.044\), \(\beta_U = 0.028\);
\(R^2 = 3.3\%\).

\para{A9. Taker fee} \(f(q) = r\,q(1-q)\) a share at price \(q\), or \(r(1-q)\) per dollar, which is
\(10{,}000\,r(1-q)\) bps. At \(r = 5\%\): \(q = 0.5\) pays 1.25¢
(250\,bps) and \(q = 0.8\) pays 0.80¢ (100\,bps).

\para{A10. Point swing} \(\Delta(k)\) of Section~\ref{sec:hyp}, with the match-win probability computed exactly by recursion over points,
games, tiebreaks and sets (\texttt{src/markov.py}). Two equal players who win 0.645 of service points:
at the first point \(\Pr(\text{win}) = 50.0\%\), moving to 51.2\% or
47.8\%, so \(\Delta = 3.4\%\). Serving at 5--5, 30--40 in the deciding
set, \(\Pr(\text{win}) = 33.7\%\), moving to 47.3\% or 8.9\%, so
\(\Delta = 38.4\%\). The mean \(|\Delta|\) over a match is 5.7\%.

\para{A11. Latency budget} Eq.~\ref{eq:budget}. With the pre-registered \(L = 2.0\)\,s, \(D =
1\)\,s and our measured \(\delta = 0.119\)\,s, a point whose book reprices at the
median \(R = −1.16\)\,s needs \(V < −0.28 + \ell\): no feed wins it
without an early call. At the 90th percentile, \(R = 0.08\)\,s and \(V < 0.96\)\,s.
The sweep's break-even (1.09\,s IS) comes from these late reprices.

\para{A12. Capital and return on capital} Capital is three times the peak dollars locked in open positions,
each held 4\,h or until resolution. v2 IS: peak \$9,434, capital \$28,302; return \(= 365\,\bar
d/\text{capital} = 365 \times \$196.24 / \$28,302 = 253\%\) a year.

\para{A13. Capacity and volume share} Capacity is the capital at which the seed-mean Sharpe falls to half its
smallest-size value; share is our notional a day over market volume a day. CV, post hoc, IS, just below half Sharpe:
\$10,428 a day against \$4.71M of in-play volume (0.22\%) and
\$117,047 traded by the fast tier 0--3\,s after a point (9\%).
