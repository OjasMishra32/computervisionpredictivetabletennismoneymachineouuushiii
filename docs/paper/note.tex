\begin{csabstract}
We study who profits from speed in Polymarket's in-play tennis moneylines (13,084 matches, public
tapes). A walk-forward fast tier of wallets trading within 3\,s of a score move earns after fees in every in-sample
and held-out month; other takers lose, and copying them 3\,s later loses. At the fast tier's own fills a
frozen book (v2) has a Sharpe ratio of 14.5 in sample and 6.7 on a burned hold-out,
but turns negative when fees double. We then price the speed a computer-vision (CV) trader needs (simulated;
assumed feed latency (licensed feed not purchased); parameters measured). Pre-registered, it breaks even at a 1.0–1.1\,s feed delay and earns
+\$15, +\$4 and −\$13 a day held out at 0.5, 1
and 3\,s; a post hoc stamp-lag inference gives +\$57 at 1\,s. A replay on real books loses.
Real money: none.

\vspace{2pt}\noindent\textbf{JEL:} G14, G12, C58.\enspace \textbf{Keywords:} prediction markets; latency arbitrage;
in-play betting; tennis; computer vision; market microstructure.
\end{csabstract}

% ======================================================================================== 2 Hypothesis
\section{Economic hypothesis}\label{sec:hyp}
Win probability is a known function of the score. The leverage of point \(k\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lev}
  \Delta(k) = \Pr(\text{win}\mid \text{point } k \text{ won}) - \Pr(\text{win}\mid \text{point } k \text{ lost}),
\end{equation}
follows from the point-level Markov chain \citep{klaassen2001}; mean \(|\Delta|\) is 5.7\%. Each point
moves fair value by a known amount at a known instant, and the news reaches traders in tiers (Fig.~\ref{fig:race}a);
stale makers are picked off by whoever learns first \citep{glosten1985,budish2015,aquilina2022}. The rent is being
competed away: the fast tier grew from 4 to 131 wallets as its edge
fell 0.20¢ a month (\(t = −3.98\)). In-play mispricing is known
\citep{croxson2014,brown2012}; new here: a wallet-level map of the latency tier and a price on each second.
\begin{hypbox}
\textbf{H6.} \emph{Universe:} Polymarket ATP/WTA/Challenger moneylines. \emph{Behaviour:} wallets trading 0--3\,s
after a score move. \emph{Horizon:} 30\,s markout. \emph{Counterparty:} the resting maker who has not yet cancelled a
stale quote. \emph{Persistence:} physics and data licences decide who learns first; the 1\,s taker delay lets makers
cancel (the cost \(D\) in Eq.~\ref{eq:budget}). \emph{Prediction:} fast wallets chosen on past months keep a positive
net markout each month and out of sample. \emph{Falsifier:} \(\leq 0\) in over a third of months, or out of sample.
\emph{Trail:} H1--H4 committed before any result (\texttt{7232986}); H6 frozen before the OOS opened.
\end{hypbox}

% ======================================================================================== 3 Data
\section{Data and universe}\label{sec:data}
13,084 resolved ATP/WTA/Challenger singles moneylines with \(\geq\)\$5,000 volume,
Oct 8, 2025 to Oct 3, 2026, public books and tapes \citep{pmapi}. IS: the first 80\% of matches by
start; OOS: the last 2,617 from 2026-08-25 14:15 UTC (burned for v2 and the CV simulation). A blind
test on 11,307 smaller markets checks survivorship; 6 tapes may be truncated,
2,889 early matches pay no fee, 2.89\% settle 50/50 (kept), and tape stamps lag
1.98\,s. Timing: ESPN, WTA \citep{espn,wtaapi}; vision: OpenTTGames
\citep{voeikov2020}, BlurBall and WASB weights \citep{gossard2026,tarashima2023}.

% ======================================================================================== 4 Methodology
\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}
\para{Fast tier and v2} A detector fires on a \(\geq\)4¢ move of the 10\,s VWAP against the prior
60\,s. Each month a wallet qualifies on past months only (\(\geq\)30 prints 0--3\,s after a
detection, \(\geq\)10 matches, \(t > 3\)). The frozen book v2 copies their
prints, sized by resolution risk, at most \$1,000 an order and 100 shares net per
match, held to resolution, at the fast tier's own prices: \emph{the opportunity at their speed, not our execution}
(components: Table~\ref{tab:comp}).

\para{CV and the latency budget} Frame to unsigned order takes 54\,ms; with a simulated 1\,s feed,
65\,ms network and the 1\,s venue hold an order is executable at 2,119\,ms, inside the
organiser's 3\,s bar (Fig.~\ref{fig:f2t}; paper, no order sent). It wins only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
  V + \delta + D - \ell \;<\; L + R \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V \;<\; L + R - D - \delta + \ell ,
\end{equation}
where \(V\) is the feed delay, \(\delta\) our pipeline and network, \(D\) the 1\,s taker delay,
\(\ell\) the call lead, \(L\) the stamp lag and \(R\) the reprice relative to the stamp (measured, \(n =
482\)). The trade set is the historical \(\geq\)4¢ detector set,
\emph{selected on outcomes, not ex ante}; the replay calls every point. \(L\) is unmeasured; Table~\ref{tab:head} shows
every reading. Only \(L + R\) enters, so \(V = 1\)\,s at the post hoc \(L = 3.14\)\,s equals a camera
0.14\,s ahead of the point at \(L = 2.0\)\,s.

\para{Costs} Each match's own taker fee, \(f(q) = r\,q\,(1-q)\) per share \(= 10{,}000\,r\,(1-q)\) bps of notional,
\(r = 5\%\) today; spreads are paid as traded; Table~\ref{tab:fail} doubles them. \para{Validation} Walk-forward inside IS; the fee in force; every read of held-out data logged.

% ======================================================================================== 5 Results
\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
The fast tier beats other takers every month and copying it 3\,s later loses (Fig.~\ref{fig:edge}a). v2 earns in
both periods (Table~\ref{tab:head}, Fig.~\ref{fig:edge}b); out of sample the edge is gone at ½ tick worse entry
(+0.10¢) and negative with fees doubled (−0.34¢).
\para{Sharpe above 3} We assumed a bug and found one: fixing the onset look-ahead (D9) cut IS Sharpe from
16.8 to 14.5. The rest is about 270 small, nearly
independent bets a day. The deflated Sharpe \citep{bailey2014} is 0.997 IS but 0.075
OOS: 40 days cannot rule out luck.
\para{Latency scenarios} Pre-registered, the CV trader makes +\$15, +\$4
and −\$13 a day held out at 0.5, 1 and 3\,s (Table~\ref{tab:head}B, Fig.~\ref{fig:speed}); post
hoc, +\$81, +\$57 and −\$11, but at its
interval's low end only +\$8. The replay calls every point ex ante and loses in
36 of 36 settings (Fig.~\ref{fig:replay}b): the gap is the value of knowing in
advance which points matter, which no tested rule supplies yet.

% ======================================================================================== 6 Risk
\section{Risk management}\label{sec:risk}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Limits, in code; de-risking, set in advance.} \$1,000 per order,
100 shares net per match, zone 0.05–0.95: one match can lose at most
\$95 plus fees. Kill switches: stale feed \(>\)2\,s, vision
\(>\)1\,s, latency above its rolling p95; a \$1,000 daily stop. Halve
size if the trailing 30-day edge is below 0.3¢; stop at \(\leq 0\), a 5\%
drawdown, or a fee or delay change.
\item \textbf{CV model risk.} Wrong calls always fill; MISS calls trade only at confidence \(\geq\)0.95, and a
confirmed false call halts the day. Phantom calls outside play (about 30 an hour) would erase the
1\,s OOS P\&L at 2.5 (pre-registered) or 32 (post hoc) a day; a
rally-state gate is coded but not yet evaluated.
\item \textbf{Tail, venue, legal.} The UMA oracle settles
\citep{pmuma}; US persons are close-only \citep{pmgeoblock}; courtsiding breaches ticket terms \citep{itf2026}.
Factors \citep{fama1993,carhart1997}: \(\alpha\) \(t = 8.48\), max \(|t| = 1.36\).
\end{itemize}

% ======================================================================================== 7 Liquidity
\section{Liquidity and capacity}\label{sec:liq}
v2 trades \$7,182 a day, 0.07\% of match volume; its OOS CI excludes zero
only up to 1\(\times\) size (\$22,754) and 5\(\times\) loses. The CV book at the pre-registered lag
has Sharpe 2.7\,/\,0.8 (IS\,/\,OOS) at its smallest size: no capacity.
Post hoc, its Sharpe halves at \$40,000 of capital OOS and \$73,000 IS
(Fig.~\ref{fig:capacity}); \$/day peaks at \$177 on \$83,000 OOS (Sharpe
1.3). It trades 0.22\% of in-play volume but 9\%
of the fast tier's 0--3\,s volume: the seconds after each point bind. The cheapest data
stack (\$42 a day) needs \$14,000--\$16,000 of capital post hoc; at
1\,s the book can pay at most \$1,644 a month for data post hoc and \$55
pre-registered, against quotes of \$1,250--\$10,000. At today's fee COURTSIDE prices
speed; it is not yet a business.

% ======================================================================================== 8 Limitations
\section{Limitations and next steps}\label{sec:limits}
One number, the stamp lag \(L\), moves the break-even one for one (Eq.~\ref{eq:budget}); its post hoc value assumes
human reaction, but the 1\,s hold dominates (\(L \geq 2.85\)\,s even for a 20\,ms bot). One session with
a licensed feed and test orders measures \(L\) and whether delayed orders are released on a 1\,s clock. Licensing that
feed is a purchase decision, not yet a deployment: sub-second video is sold to licensed sportsbooks, and at 1\,s the
book covers the cheapest data quote only if \(L \geq 3.0\)\,s. CV accuracy is from table tennis; tennis
is simulated; no order was ever sent.\label{lastmain}

\section*{Calculations}\label{sec:calc}
Every statistic in the paper, its formula and one worked example with our numbers (source keys in
\texttt{results/paper/numbers.json}).

\para{C1. Annualised Sharpe} \(\text{SR} = \sqrt{365}\,\bar d / s_d\), where \(d_t\) is the daily net P\&L on every
calendar day of the period (zero on days without trades) and \(s_d\) its standard deviation (\(n-1\)). v2 IS:
\(\bar d = \$196.24\), \(s_d = \$258.87\), \(T = 206\) days, so
\(\text{SR}_\text{d} = 0.758\) and \(\text{SR} = 0.758 \times 19.105 =
14.48\). OOS: \(\$92.19 / \$264.15 \times 19.105 =
6.67\).

\para{C2. Sortino} \(\sqrt{365}\,\bar d / \text{DD}\), \(\text{DD} = \sqrt{\tfrac1T\sum_t \min(d_t,0)^2}\). v2 OOS:
\(\text{DD} = \$125.64\), so \(\sqrt{365}\times \$92.19 / \$125.64 =
14.0\).

\para{C3. Maximum drawdown} \(\text{MDD} = \min_t \big(E_t - \max_{s\le t} E_s\big)\), \(E_t = \sum_{u\le t} d_u\),
reported as a share of capital. v2 OOS: \(−\$469 / \$22,754 = −2.06\%\).

\para{C4. Probabilistic Sharpe} \(\text{PSR}(\text{SR}^*) = \Phi\!\Big((\widehat{\text{SR}}-\text{SR}^*)\sqrt{T-1}\,\big/\sqrt{1-\gamma_3\widehat{\text{SR}}+\tfrac{\gamma_4-1}{4}\widehat{\text{SR}}^2}\Big)\)
on daily SR, skew \(\gamma_3\) and kurtosis \(\gamma_4\) \citep{bailey2014}. v2 OOS: \(\widehat{\text{SR}} =
0.349\), \(T = 40\), \(\gamma_3 = 0.326\), \(\gamma_4 =
3.26\): \(\text{PSR}(0) = 0.987\).

\para{C5. Deflated Sharpe} \(\text{DSR} = \text{PSR}(\text{SR}_0)\) with \(\text{SR}_0 = \sqrt{V}\big((1-\gamma)\Phi^{-1}(1-\tfrac1N) + \gamma\,\Phi^{-1}(1-\tfrac{1}{Ne})\big)\),
\(\gamma\) the Euler--Mascheroni constant and \(N\) the trial count. v2 OOS, \(N = 3,386\), null
variance \(V = 1/(T-1)\): \(\Phi^{-1}\) terms 3.436 and 3.698, \(\text{SR}_0 = 0.574\)
daily (10.97 annualised), DSR \(= 0.075\). Table~\ref{tab:head} reports the lowest DSR
over three variance sources.

\para{C6. Probability of backtest overfitting} CSCV \citep{bailey2017}: split the daily P\&L of \(24\)
variants into \(S = 16\) blocks; for each of the \(\binom{16}{8} = 12,870\)
halvings pick the IS-best variant and take the logit \(\lambda = \ln\frac{\omega}{1-\omega}\) of its OOS rank
\(\omega\); PBO \(= \Pr(\lambda \le 0)\). v2-safe grid: PBO \(= 15\%\) (median \(\lambda =
1.27\)).

\para{C7. Stationary block bootstrap} \citep{politis1994}: resample the daily series in blocks of geometric length
(mean 5 days), 10,000 draws; percentile CI. v2 OOS Sharpe: [1.9, 12.3], standard error
2.65, \(P(\text{SR}\le 0) = 0.0023\).

\para{C8. Factor regression} \(r_t = \alpha + \beta_M\,\text{MktRF}_t + \beta_S\,\text{SMB}_t + \beta_H\,\text{HML}_t +
\beta_U\,\text{Mom}_t + \varepsilon_t\) on daily return on capital, Newey--West (5 lags) \citep{fama1993,carhart1997,newey1987}.
v2 IS (138 days): \(\alpha = 0.801\%\) a day (\(t = 8.48\)); \(\beta_M =
0.173\) (\(t = 1.36\)), \(\beta_S = 0.085\), \(\beta_H =
0.044\), \(\beta_U = 0.028\); \(R^2 = 3.3\%\).

\para{C9. Taker fee} \(f(q) = r\,q(1-q)\) per share at price \(q\); per dollar of notional \(f/q = r(1-q)\), i.e.
\(10{,}000\,r(1-q)\) bps. At \(r = 5\%\): \(q = 0.5\) pays 1.25¢ a share
(250\,bps); \(q = 0.8\) pays 0.80¢ (100\,bps).

\para{C10. Markov point leverage} Eq.~\ref{eq:lev}, with the match won probability computed exactly by recursion
over points, games, tiebreaks and sets (\texttt{src/markov.py}). Two even players who win 0.645 of
service points: at the first point \(\Pr(\text{win}) = 50.0\%\), moving to
51.2\% or 47.8\%, so \(\Delta = 3.4\%\); serving at
5--5, 30--40 in the deciding set, \(\Pr(\text{win}) = 33.7\%\), moving to 47.3\%
or 8.9\%: \(\Delta = 38.4\%\). Mean \(|\Delta|\) over a match:
5.7\%.

\para{C11. Latency budget} Eq.~\ref{eq:budget}. With the pre-registered \(L = 2.0\)\,s, \(D =
1\)\,s and our measured \(\delta = 0.119\)\,s (pipeline plus network), a point
whose book reprices at the median \(R = −1.16\)\,s needs \(V < −0.28
+ \ell\): no feed wins it without an early call. At the 90th percentile \(R = 0.08\)\,s, \(V <
0.96\)\,s; the sweep's break-even (1.09\,s IS) comes from such late reprices.

\para{C12. Capital and return on capital} Capital \(= 3\times\) the peak dollars locked in open positions (each
locked 4\,h, or until resolution). v2 IS: peak \$9,434, capital \$28,302; return \(= 365\,\bar
d/\text{capital} = 365 \times \$196.24 / \$28,302 = 253\%\) a year.

\para{C13. Capacity and volume share} Capacity is the capital at which the seed-mean Sharpe falls to half its
smallest-size value along the size path; share \(=\) our notional per day \(/\) market volume per day. CV, post hoc,
IS, just below the half-Sharpe size: \$10,428 a day against \$4.71M of in-play volume
(0.22\%) and \$117,047 traded by the fast tier 0--3\,s after a point
(9\%).
