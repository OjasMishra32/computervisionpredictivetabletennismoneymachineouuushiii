\begin{csabstract}
We built a ball-tracking engine that calls a point (ball out or into the net) before it lands: on
held-out table tennis it called 4 of 41 misses a median
162\,ms early, none wrong, at 119.9\,fps, and on broadcast tennis its out calls were
right 5 of 5 at the bounce and 3 of 3 when 33\,ms ahead. On
13,084 Polymarket ATP/WTA matches, wallets that trade within 3\,s of a point earn after fees in all
11 months, as our pre-registered hypothesis predicted out of sample. What we could trade
loses after costs: a copier of their trades, entering after the detection second, the venue's hold and the block-time
lag, makes −1.42¢ a share in sample and −1.02¢ out of sample (−\$31,
−\$28 a day; the OOS interval spans zero); a camera policy trading every point from our classifier's
measured call statistics at an assumed licensed 0.5\,s feed makes −\$92, −\$146
a day (upper-bound fills), less at 1 and 3\,s, and earns only at a post hoc score-stamp delay
(+\$174, +\$65). Untradable benchmarks price the speed we lack: the
fast tier's own fills (v2; Sharpe 14.5, 6.7; doubled fees erase the OOS gain) and a computer-vision (CV) trader on
points the market later repriced (+\$15 a day out of sample pre-registered,
+\$81 post hoc). Our one test with rules fixed in advance holds the last 20\% of matches,
10.7\% of the calendar, so no untouched test meets the organizers' by-time rule.

\vspace{2pt}\noindent\textbf{JEL:} G14, G12, C58.\enspace \textbf{Keywords:} computer vision; ball tracking; prediction
markets; latency arbitrage; in-play betting; tennis.
\end{csabstract}

% ======================================================================================== 1 Summary
\section{Introduction and summary}\label{sec:summary}
A camera films the court; a model finds the ball, fits its flight and calls the point before the bounce
(Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}). A Polymarket price \emph{is} the chance that a player wins and lags each point by about a
second, so whoever learns the point first sets the price. We map who is fast, price each second of feed delay
(\$42–74 a day for the first second, on points that later moved) and test what we could trade.

\para{Why the benchmark Sharpe ratios exceed 3} v2's 14.5 comes from about
270 small bets a day placed just after points, held to resolution, with a mean same-day
pairwise correlation of 0.0013, so daily P\&L is rarely negative. It is priced at the fast tier's own
fills, and 97.7\% of its in-sample profit (99.2\% OOS) is in prints stamped in the
second the score move was detected: what their speed earns, not what a follower can (on later prints only:
+0.33¢, +0.76¢ a share, both CIs spanning zero). Checking it, we found and fixed a detector
start time that used later prices (in-sample Sharpe 16.8 to 14.5).

% ======================================================================================== 2 Hypothesis
\section{Economic hypothesis}\label{sec:hyp}
A Markov model of the score gives the exact chance that each player wins from any score
\citep{klaassen2001}, so each point moves fair value by a known amount at a known moment (the \emph{swing}: mean
5.7\%, 38.4\% on a late break point). News reaches traders in tiers: court, video, then
score feeds 28.2--44.1\,s after the price moved. A maker whose quote
rests after the point is picked off by whoever knows first \citep{glosten1985,budish2015,aquilina2022}, as
courtsiders do in betting markets \citep{croxson2014,brown2012}: on 482 live points a median
\$222 of quotes rested at stale prices 0.25\,s before the reprice
(\$0 after 0.5\,s), and Polymarket holds each taker order 1\,s.

\begin{hypbox}
\textbf{H6 (pre-registered).} We expect wallets that trade within 3\,s of a score move in Polymarket ATP and WTA match
markets to earn a positive net \emph{markout} over 30\,s (profit at the price 30\,s later, after fees), because the
other side is a maker who has not yet cancelled a stale quote; the edge persists because physics and data licences
decide who learns first. Wallets picked on past months then stay positive every month and out of sample; H6 fails if
the markout is \(\leq 0\) in over a third of months, or out of sample. H1--H4 were committed before any result
(\texttt{7232986}); H6, written after the in-sample tier results, was frozen before any
held-out read.
\end{hypbox}

\textbf{Will the edge last?}\enspace The fast tier's net edge fell 0.16¢ a month (CI
0.06–0.25), mostly as the fee rose; a fee above 8.4\% (today 5\%) would end it.

% ======================================================================================== 3 Data
\section{Data and universe}\label{sec:data}
Every resolved Polymarket ATP/WTA singles match market with at least \$5,000 traded:
13,084 matches (1,310 at Grand Slams; \$2.84B), Oct 2025--Oct 2026,
from public APIs \citep{pmapi}, point in time, so no match is dropped by outcome: 378
settled 50/50 (302 never started) and 283 ended in a retirement, all at
settlement price. \emph{In sample} (IS) is the first 80\% of matches by start time, \emph{out of sample} (OOS) the last
2,617, from 2026-08-25 14:15 UTC, a match-count split pre-registered before any result
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}). Other data: Table~\ref{tab:data}
\citep{huang2019,voeikov2020,sackmann2026}.

\para{Data problems} Six tapes hit the API's row cap (kept); 2,889 early matches had no published fee
schedule (charged none); tape times are block times 1.98\,s late; gaps are carried forward, never
filled from later prints; no corporate actions apply. Regimes: the taker hold fell from 3\,s to 1\,s on
May 15 and the fee rose from 3\% to 5\%; v2 is positive in each (+1.96¢ to
+1.02¢ IS), and the CV trader is simulated only on the 7,755 matches with the 1\,s hold.

% ======================================================================================== 4 Methodology
\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}
\para{The fast tier and v2} Each month a wallet joins the fast tier if, in earlier months, it traded at least
30 times 0--3\,s after a score move (a \(\geq\)4¢ move of the 10\,s price
from its prior 60\,s) in 10+ matches (markout \(t > 3\)).
v2 books their trades at their own fill prices, sized by resolution risk (best of 55 rules,
walk-forward), with \$1,000 orders and 100 shares net per match, held to resolution. Our
\emph{copier} takes only v2 trades printed after the \emph{detection second} (tapes stamp whole seconds, unordered within
one) and fills after that print, the network leg, the venue's hold and the median 1.98\,s block-time lag,
at the next same-side print; fills past the tape's end are rejected (1 IS,
1 OOS). New here: picking wallets by speed relative to the point, the sizing and limits,
and the copier test.

\para{Being fast ourselves} A CV model watches the video, calls the point early and sends a taker order before the stale
quote is cancelled. We simulate it on past points with an assumed feed latency; all
other parameters are measured. The order wins only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
  V + \delta + D - \ell \;<\; L + R \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V \;<\; L + R - D - \delta + \ell ,
\end{equation}
with \(V\) the feed delay, \(\delta\) our pipeline plus network, \(D\) the 1\,s hold, \(\ell\) how
early the CV calls, \(L\) the \emph{stamp lag} (landing to official stamp) and \(R\) the reprice relative to the stamp
(median −1.16\,s), drawn from its measured spread once per tournament (the headline
reading) or, as pre-registered, per point. Nobody publishes \(L\): we \emph{pre-registered}
2.0\,s and also show 3.14\,s, a \emph{post hoc} estimate from one day. The
\emph{causal policy} trades every point plus the classifier's false calls, each order chosen, signed and sized from
what is known before it. We have no footage of the Polymarket matches, so the frozen classifier made no real calls on
them: the policy draws calls from its measured table-tennis statistics (recall by lead, precision, 8.5 false
calls an hour); fills use the post-point price and the live book, an upper bound. The \emph{CV benchmark} trades only
points the price later moved \(\geq\)4¢ on, so it selects on the outcome.

\para{Vision and speed} On broadcast tennis only out calls at the bounce met our 95\% precision rule in
training (test: 3 of 4 right 67\,ms ahead). Our live engine makes about 30 phantom calls an hour outside play
(8 with a rally gate): not yet safe to trade. With a simulated 1\,s feed, our 54\,ms
pipeline, 65\,ms of network and the hold, an order executes 2,119\,ms after the point
(p99 2,218\,ms; not sent), within 3\,s. A London server saw
99.74\% of 287,577 book messages before a Florida one; with measured cold round
trips (not order latency) the order executes in
2,089--2,146\,ms (2,512\,ms at worst).

\para{Costs} Each match charges its own taker fee \(f(q) = r\,q(1-q)\) a share, \(10{,}000\,r(1-q)\) bps
of notional \citep[fee schedule]{pmapi}; today \(r = 5\%\), 250\,bps at 50¢.
Fees paid were 120 and 179\,bps of notional for v2 (IS, OOS) and
163–226\,bps for the CV trader; fills cross the real spread (CV: walk the measured stale book).
The stress doubles the fee (v2: also the spread).

\para{How we ran the tests} The OOS period was evaluated once, on
Oct 3 at 10:42 UTC, for the hypotheses and v1 book frozen in \texttt{9d61d1b}; all later work
knew that OOS, so its OOS figures are \emph{non-blind}. Our experiment registry traces 2
strategy designs to OOS results (v2, after v1's OOS loss; the per-wallet cap, after the OOS concentration; both fitted
in sample), 4 presentation choices made after OOS cells existed (timing reading, post hoc lag, 1\,s
base case, 0.5\,s main scenario; alternatives printed), 1 unexplained variant adoption and
4 mechanical corrections applied to every period (Appendix~D).

\para{The copy book and overfitting checks} The fast tier earns every month and everyone else loses
(Fig.~\ref{fig:edge}a); at their own fills v2 earns in both periods, thinly, while the copier nets
−\$6,351 IS and −\$1,104 [−\$3,221, +\$900] OOS. Six tests on
daily P\&L (probabilistic Sharpe and minimum track record against Sharpe 0 and 2, \citealp{bailey2012}; a Bonferroni
haircut over all 4,219 variants, \citealp{harvey2015}; a 5-day block bootstrap): v2 passes all six in
sample (haircut Sharpe 13.4) but 3 on the 40-day OOS
(\(t = 2.21\) vs 4.38). OOS, the CV benchmark fails all six at the pre-registered
lag and passes 4 post hoc: none passes all six out of sample. Nearby settings work: all
55 sizing rules have an in-sample per-share CI above zero; the probability of backtest
overfitting is 9–24\%.

\para{What speed is worth} Trading every point causally, the camera policy loses at every feed delay at the
pre-registered lag (with \(R\) drawn per point: −\$136, −\$190 a day at 0.5\,s).
The post hoc 3.14\,s lag earns at 0.5\,s but loses OOS at 1 and 3\,s (−\$45,
−\$227); doubled fees make every causal cell lose. A replay of 9
live-recorded matches calling every point loses in 36 of 36 settings
(−0.66¢ at 0.5\,s). On points that later moved, the pre-registered benchmark breaks even at a
1.01\,s delay OOS (Table~\ref{tab:A-cal}); doubled fees erase it (best OOS
−\$13): the gap is knowing which points matter.

% ======================================================================================== 7 Liquidity
\section{Liquidity and capacity}\label{sec:liq}
v2 holds its OOS edge up to \$22,754 of capital (at 5\(\times\) it loses), trading
0.07\% of match volume; the CV benchmark's Sharpe halves at
\$40,000--\$73,000 post hoc (Fig.~\ref{fig:capacity}). With price impact fitted to
8.4 million IS orders, v2 at 1\(\times\) earns \$88 a day OOS, not
\$92 (\$71 conservatively), and CV capacity moves under
1\%; the simulation takes half the stale depth; other takers took only
4–11\% of it in live books. After fixed data and running costs v2 makes
+\$29 a day IS and −\$75 OOS: not yet a business.

% ======================================================================================== 8 Limitations
\section{Limitations and the out-of-sample rule}\label{sec:limits}
What earns is a benchmark or needs the post hoc lag; the camera policy uses table-tennis call statistics,
not real calls on these matches, and upper-bound fills; \(L\) is unmeasured. Our one evaluation with rules fixed in
advance used the pre-registered last 20\% of matches: 2,617 over 38.7 days,
10.7\% of the history. The organizers' rule takes the latest 20\% by time
(72.0 days from Jul 23), all read before the corrected policies were frozen;
new matches alone would need 90 days. No policy here has an untouched test
meeting the by-time rule; the frozen v1 test meets it only by match count, and every later OOS result is non-blind and
exploratory. Next: a licensed feed to measure \(L\) and make real calls, then a 90-day forward test.\label{lastmain}

\csappendix
\section*{Appendix A\quad Calculations}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Appendix}
The main pages stand alone. Below: formulas with worked examples (A), every test (B), extra figures (C) and the
records (D). Each value is in \texttt{results/paper/numbers.json} with its source file.

\para{A1. Sharpe ratio} \(\text{SR} = \sqrt{365}\,\bar d / s_d\), where \(d_t\) is net P\&L on every calendar day
(zero on days without trades) and \(s_d\) its standard deviation. v2 IS: \(\bar d = \$196.24\),
\(s_d = \$258.87\) over \(T = 206\) days, so the daily ratio is 0.758
and \(\text{SR} = 0.758 \times 19.105 = 14.48\). OOS:
\(\$92.19 / \$264.15 \times 19.105 = 6.67\).

\para{A2. Sortino ratio} \(\sqrt{365}\,\bar d / \text{DD}\), where the downside deviation is \(\text{DD} =
\sqrt{\tfrac1T\sum_t \min(d_t,0)^2}\). For v2 OOS, \(\text{DD} = \$125.64\), so the ratio is
\(\sqrt{365}\times \$92.19 / \$125.64 = 14.0\).

\para{A3. Maximum drawdown} \(\text{MDD} = \min_t \big(E_t - \max_{s\le t} E_s\big)\), where \(E_t\) is cumulative
P\&L, as a share of capital. v2 OOS: \(−\$469 / \$22,754 = −2.06\%\).

\para{A4. Probabilistic Sharpe} The chance that the true daily Sharpe beats \(\text{SR}^*\), given the sample length
\(T\), skew \(\gamma_3\) and kurtosis \(\gamma_4\) \citep{bailey2014}:
\[\text{PSR}(\text{SR}^*) = \Phi\Big((\widehat{\text{SR}}-\text{SR}^*)\sqrt{T-1}\,\Big/\sqrt{1-\gamma_3\widehat{\text{SR}}+\tfrac{\gamma_4-1}{4}\widehat{\text{SR}}^2}\Big).\]
v2 OOS: \(\widehat{\text{SR}} = 0.349\), \(T = 40\),
\(\gamma_3 = 0.326\), \(\gamma_4 = 3.26\), so \(\text{PSR}(0) = 0.987\).

\para{A5. Deflated Sharpe} \(\text{DSR} = \text{PSR}(\text{SR}_0)\), where \(\text{SR}_0\) is the best Sharpe we would
expect from \(N\) tries on pure noise, with \(\gamma\) the Euler--Mascheroni constant:
\[\text{SR}_0 = \sqrt{V}\Big((1-\gamma)\,\Phi^{-1}\big(1-\tfrac1N\big) + \gamma\,\Phi^{-1}\big(1-\tfrac{1}{Ne}\big)\Big).\] v2 OOS with \(N =
3,386\) and \(V = 1/(T-1)\): the two \(\Phi^{-1}\) terms are 3.436 and 3.698,
\(\text{SR}_0 = 0.574\) a day (10.97 a year), and DSR \(= 0.075\).
Table~\ref{tab:head} shows the lowest DSR over three choices of \(V\). \(N = 3,386\) is our variant
count when the deflation ran; the count is now 4,219, which the haircut of Section~\ref{sec:results}
uses (at that \(N\) the OOS haircut is already 0).

\para{A6. Probability of backtest overfitting} \citep{bailey2017}. Split the daily P\&L of 24
variants into \(S = 16\) blocks. For each of the \(\binom{16}{8} = 12,870\) ways
to call half the blocks in sample, take the best in-sample variant, find its rank \(\omega\) out of sample and set
\(\lambda = \ln\frac{\omega}{1-\omega}\); PBO \(= \Pr(\lambda \le 0)\). v2-safe grid: PBO \(= 15\%\)
(median \(\lambda = 1.27\)).

\para{A7. Stationary block bootstrap} \citep{politis1994}. Resample days in blocks of random length (mean 5 days)
10,000 times and read the percentiles. v2 OOS Sharpe: [1.9, 12.3], standard error 2.65,
\(P(\text{SR}\le 0) = 0.0023\).

\para{A8. Factor regression} \(r_t = \alpha + \beta_M\,\text{MktRF}_t + \beta_S\,\text{SMB}_t + \beta_H\,\text{HML}_t
+ \beta_U\,\text{Mom}_t + \varepsilon_t\) on daily return on capital, with Newey--West errors (5 lags)
\citep{fama1993,carhart1997,newey1987,french}. v2 IS (138 days): \(\alpha = 0.801\%\) a day
(\(t = 8.48\)); \(\beta_M = 0.173\) (\(t = 1.36\)),
\(\beta_S = 0.085\), \(\beta_H = 0.044\), \(\beta_U = 0.028\);
\(R^2 = 3.3\%\).

\para{A9. Taker fee} \(f(q) = r\,q(1-q)\) a share at price \(q\), or \(r(1-q)\) per dollar, which is
\(10{,}000\,r(1-q)\) bps. At \(r = 5\%\): \(q = 0.5\) pays 1.25¢
(250\,bps) and \(q = 0.8\) pays 0.80¢ (100\,bps).

\para{A10. Point swing} \(\Delta(k)\) of Section~\ref{sec:hyp}, with the match-win probability computed exactly by recursion over points,
games, tiebreaks and sets (\texttt{src/markov.py}). Two equal players who win 0.645 of service points:
at the first point \(\Pr(\text{win}) = 50.0\%\), moving to 51.2\% or
47.8\%, so \(\Delta = 3.4\%\). Serving at 5--5, 30--40 in the deciding
set, \(\Pr(\text{win}) = 33.7\%\), moving to 47.3\% or 8.9\%, so
\(\Delta = 38.4\%\). The mean \(|\Delta|\) over a match is 5.7\%.

\para{A11. Latency budget} Eq.~\ref{eq:budget}. With the pre-registered \(L = 2.0\)\,s, \(D =
1\)\,s and our measured \(\delta = 0.119\)\,s, a point whose book reprices at the
median \(R = −1.16\)\,s needs \(V < −0.28 + \ell\): no feed wins it
without an early call. At the 90th percentile, \(R = 0.08\)\,s and \(V < 0.96\)\,s.
The sweep's break-even (1.09\,s IS) comes from these late reprices.

\para{A12. Capital and return on capital} Capital is three times the peak dollars locked in open positions,
each held 4\,h or until resolution. v2 IS: peak \$9,434, capital \$28,302; return \(= 365\,\bar
d/\text{capital} = 365 \times \$196.24 / \$28,302 = 253\%\) a year.

\para{A13. Capacity and volume share} Capacity is the capital at which the seed-mean Sharpe falls to half its
smallest-size value; share is our notional a day over market volume a day. CV, post hoc, IS, just below half Sharpe:
\$10,428 a day against \$4.71M of in-play volume (0.22\%) and
\$117,047 traded by the fast tier 0--3\,s after a point (9\%).
